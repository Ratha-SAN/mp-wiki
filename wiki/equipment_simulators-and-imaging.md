## Synthesis

**Scope of this page.** Covers the imaging/simulation equipment specified in WHO/IAEA's *Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment* (2021) (MP Library/Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment.pdf) — CT simulators and conventional simulators for EBRT treatment planning (Sections 3.4–3.5), and C-arm fluoroscopic X-ray units and ultrasound imaging systems for brachytherapy applicator placement (Sections 4.3–4.4). See [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]] for the treatment delivery units these imaging systems support.

**CT simulator.** A dedicated large-bore (≥80 cm diameter, vs. a standard diagnostic scanner's 70 cm bore) whole-body spiral/multi-slice (≥16 slices/rotation) CT scanner fitted with a flat carbon-fibre couch top and an external laser system (set back ~600 mm from the image plane, intersecting at the mechanical isocentre) — the large bore accommodates patients in extended/tilted/laterally-offset treatment positions using immobilization devices, which a standard diagnostic-bore scanner cannot. Key specified performance: ≤1 mm positioning accuracy, sub-millimetre-to-8 mm slice thickness, ≤0.6 s/rotation scan time, 512×512 reconstruction matrix, ≥15 lp/cm high-contrast spatial resolution at 0% MTF, ≤5 mm low-contrast detectability, CT number accuracy 0±4 HU (water)/−1000±10 HU (air). Patient CT data transfer to the TPS uses the DICOM standard. Where a dedicated CT simulator is unaffordable (Package 1 settings), the report explicitly permits substituting a shared diagnostic radiology CT scanner fitted with the flat-top couch and external laser accessories, with a radiation therapist present at scan time and direct data transfer to the TPS — an explicitly resource-constrained fallback, not the preferred configuration. A cone-beam "sim-CT" volumetric mode is available on conventional simulators but is explicitly excluded from this specification due to inferior image quality relative to true CT.

**Conventional simulator.** Mechanically mirrors a LINAC/cobalt-60 unit (isocentric gantry ±180°, ≤2.0 mm isocentre sphere, adjustable focus-to-isocentre distance 80–100 cm to match the department's treatment units) but replaces the megavoltage treatment source with a diagnostic-energy X-ray tube (≤125 kVp, ≤300 mAs radiography / ≤4 mA fluoroscopy) for planar 2D simulation/verification imaging — supporting 2D EBRT planning and offline verification/markup for 3D-CRT, but not itself a 3D imaging device. Includes radio-opaque field-defining wires independent of the collimator diaphragm (for marking intended treatment fields on the image), a flat-panel digital imaging system (≥30×30 cm), and the same four-laser external alignment set as the CT simulator and treatment units. A conventional simulator can also be used for brachytherapy 2D treatment planning (see below).

**C-arm fluoroscopic X-ray unit (brachytherapy imaging).** A mobile C-arm-mounted X-ray source/imaging system providing planar imaging of the brachytherapy patient with applicators already inserted, dedicated to brachytherapy rather than shared with EBRT simulation — its stated advantage over a shared conventional simulator or CT simulator. Limited to 2D planning only (a reconstruction box is needed to derive 3D dwell-position geometry from multiple 2D projections). Specified: 40–110 kV generator, ≥21×21 cm flat-panel detector, ≥120° C-arm orbital travel (−30° to +90°), ≥40 cm vertical / ≥20 cm horizontal motorized travel, DICOM 3.0 export to the brachytherapy TPS or hospital PACS. Radiation safety is simpler than for a dedicated X-ray room: the unit operates only inside the already-shielded brachytherapy treatment room (see [[equipment_HDR-brachytherapy-afterloader]]), with the mobile-unit operator requiring a lead apron/thyroid collar during exposure (other staff must leave the room) rather than requiring separate room shielding calculations.

**Ultrasound imaging system.** Used specifically to guide and confirm intracavitary applicator placement during brachytherapy (not for EBRT). Specified: 2D and M-mode capability, two active transducer ports, high-frequency (2.0–5.0 MHz) convex transabdominal transducers as standard, with biplane transrectal transducers (3.0–12.0 MHz, sagittal and transverse) as a Package 2 addition — the higher-frequency transrectal option giving finer near-field resolution for interstitial/intracavitary applicator confirmation at the cost of penetration depth. ≥256 grey levels in 2D mode; built-in measurement tools (length/height/width/angle/area/volume) support direct clinical use during applicator placement rather than purely diagnostic imaging.

**Common design threads across all four devices.** Every device in this page shares the same three structural requirements the report imposes throughout: (1) couch/table indexing compatible with the department's immobilization system (see [[equipment_immobilization-and-mould-room]]) so that a patient's setup is reproducible across simulation and treatment units; (2) DICOM (or DICOM RT) connectivity to the relevant TPS — see [[equipment_treatment-planning-and-information-systems]] — since none of these devices function as a standalone imaging tool in the radiotherapy workflow; and (3) the IEC 61217 coordinate/movement/scale convention applied consistently across simulators and treatment units, so that geometric parameters (gantry angle, couch position) transfer unambiguously between simulation and delivery.
