## Synthesis

**Note on provenance.** All sources for this page live in the externally-connected `MP Library/` folder, not the wiki's own immutable `raw/` archive, so citations here use the `MP Library/<filename>.pdf` form rather than `raw/...`. This is the AAPM Working Group on Biological Effects of Hypofractionated Radiotherapy/SBRT's "Hypofractionated Treatment Effects in the Clinic" (HyTEC) series, published as a set of companion papers in *Int J Radiat Oncol Biol Phys* Vol. 110, No. 1 (2021). Two duplicate file pairs in the source folder were not reviewed separately because they matched their counterparts exactly by byte size: `Hytec Biology1.pdf`/`Hytec organ Biology supplement doc.pdf` (vs. `HyTech Biology.pdf`) and `Hytec introcution1.pdf`/`Hytec Introduction Supplement doc.pdf` (vs. `HyTEC Introduction.pdf`).

**Overview and methodology.** HyTEC's stated goal is a pooled, model-based counterpart to QUANTEC (which covered conventional fractionation) for the hypofractionated/SBRT dose range (MP Library/HyTEC Introduction.pdf). Each organ-specific paper models either tumor control probability (TCP) or normal tissue complication probability (NTCP) — occasionally both — using Poisson or logistic dose-response models fit by maximum-likelihood estimation, with 95% confidence intervals from bootstrap or profile-likelihood methods; the recurring free parameters are TD50/TCD50 (dose for 50% response) and a slope parameter (γ50 or m). Because dose per fraction varies widely across the pooled studies, most models convert doses to BED or EQD2 (see [[concept_fractionation]]) before fitting — a step whose own validity is contested at these fraction sizes (see "Mechanism and cross-reference to LQ-model breakdown" below). The master framework the individual organ papers slot into is the Overview paper's own Table 2 (NTCP) and Table 3 (TCP), summarized by region below.

### Cranial

**Brain metastases — TCP.** For single-fraction SRS at RTOG 90-05 maximum tolerated doses (24 Gy ≤20 mm, 18 Gy 21–30 mm, 15 Gy 31–40 mm), modeled 1-year local control was 95%/75%/69% for small/medium/large lesions respectively — control falls off with lesion size at a fixed maximum-tolerated dose (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Brain and Eye.pdf). Fractionation (30 Gy/3fx or 35 Gy/5fx) improves LC for medium/large lesions relative to single-fraction dosing at the size-adjusted MTD. Melanoma histology showed a steeper dose-response than other histologies.

**Brain necrosis — NTCP.** Symptomatic radionecrosis risk after single-fraction SRS rises with the volume of brain receiving ≥12 Gy (V12Gy): approximately 10% risk at V12Gy = 5 cm³, 15% at 10 cm³, 20% at >15 cm³ (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Brain and Eye1.pdf). For fractionated SRS (3–5 fx), the analogous V14Gy thresholds give lower risk at equivalent volumes (e.g. grade 1–3 risk ~3.6%/4.8%/8.6% at 5/10/20 cm³). QUANTEC's V12Gy ≤5–10 cm³ recommendation for single-fraction SRS is judged to remain reasonable.

**Optic pathway — NTCP (radiation-induced optic neuropathy, RION).** In patients without prior radiotherapy to the optic apparatus, RION risk is <1% when optic chiasm/nerve Dmax is kept below 10 Gy (1 fx), 20 Gy (3 fx), or 25 Gy (5 fx) (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Brain and Eye2.pdf). Prior RT to the optic pathway increases risk by an order of magnitude or more at the same doses — retreatment tolerance is substantially lower than de novo tolerance here, consistent with the general retreatment-tolerance principle already noted in [[technique_re-irradiation]] and [[concept_normal-tissue-response]].

**Vestibular schwannoma — TCP.** 3–5 year tumor control was 85–93.5% for single-fraction doses of 10–13 Gy, rising to 93.6% (18 Gy/3fx) and 97.2% (25 Gy/5fx) (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Brain and Eye3.pdf) — one of the few HyTEC sites where fractionation appears to improve control at a comparable biological dose rather than merely reducing toxicity.

### Head and neck

**Reirradiation SBRT — TCP.** In previously-irradiated head and neck cancer, 35–45 Gy in 5 fractions produced better local control than <30 Gy; the paper recommends 40–50 Gy EQD2-equivalent in 5 fractions for retreatment (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Head and Neck.pdf). Modeled D50 for 2-year LC was 45.1 Gy, rising to 49.8 Gy for 3-year LC. This complements the reirradiation-pattern-of-care findings already synthesized in [[technique_re-irradiation]] with a quantitative dose-response specific to the SBRT retreatment setting.

**Carotid artery / major vessel blowout — NTCP.** Pooling 238 major-vessel Dmax values from reirradiation SBRT series, the fitted logistic model gave TD50 = 45.7 Gy (Dmax, 5 fractions, 95% CI 39.6–64.9), γ50 = 1.18, with modeled bleeding-event risk of 2% at Dmax = 20 Gy and 12% at Dmax = 30 Gy (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Head and Neck1.pdf). Nonconsecutive-day treatment and a ≥6-month interval since prior RT were both associated with lower risk. As a conservative guide (not supported by high-level dose/outcome data), RTOG 3507 uses carotid Dmax ≤42 Gy and D50% ≤32 Gy for reirradiation planning. Carotid blowout risk did not clearly correlate with reirradiation interval, tumor size, or prior RT dose in the pooled data — the dominant identified risk-reduction levers were fractionation schedule and vessel-volume sparing rather than patient selection.

### Thoracic

**Stage I NSCLC — TCP.** Fitting a regrowth model (which explicitly accounts for tumor regrowth during longer follow-up, and fit the pooled data better than LQ or universal-survival-curve models) to 1-, 2-, 3-, and 5-year local control data gave an apparent α/β of ~25 Gy (T1), ~19 Gy (T2), ~21 Gy (combined) (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Lung.pdf). To reach maximal TCP, the modeled physical doses required at the PTV periphery (80% isodose) were 43±1 Gy/3fx, 47±1 Gy/4fx, 50±1 Gy/5fx for combined T1+T2 tumors; T2 tumors required ~1.3 Gy/fraction more than T1 at the isocenter. A separate caution: centrally located tumors (within 2 cm of the tracheobronchial tree) treated with dose-escalated 3-fraction regimens showed an 8-fold increase in grade ≥3 complications — central location is a distinct dose-limiting consideration addressed by RTOG 0813-style reduced fractionation for central lesions, not by this TCP model.

**Lung parenchyma (radiation-induced lung toxicity, RILT) — NTCP.** No clear dose-volume threshold for RILT was established, but pooled data support treating mean lung dose (MLD, combined lungs) ≤8 Gy in 3–5 fractions and lung V20 <10–15% as producing a symptomatic RILT rate of <10–15% (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Lung1.pdf). Interstitial lung disease (ILD) is a major risk-modifying factor: one pooled series found grade ≥3 RILT in 32% of ILD patients vs. 3.8% overall, and some groups consider ILD a relative contraindication to lung SBRT. Total prescription dose alone did not correlate well with RILT risk across studies, likely because physical doses cluster in a narrow range (48–60 Gy in 3–5 fractions) despite fractionation differences.

### Abdominal

**Liver (primary and metastatic) — TCP.** Primary liver tumors showed high local control (93%/89%/86% at 1/2/3 years) with no clear dose-response across a wide BED10 range of 60–180 Gy (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis.pdf). Liver metastases, by contrast, showed a clear dose-response: BED10 >100 Gy gave 93% 3-year LC vs. 65% at ≤100 Gy (p<.001); the fitted TCP model gave TCD50 = 16 Gy (single-fraction-equivalent). Primary and metastatic liver disease should therefore not be treated with a single shared dose-response assumption.

**Liver/GI — NTCP.** Grade ≥3 liver enzyme toxicity did not correlate significantly with mean liver dose in the pooled analysis; grade ≥2 GI toxicity vs. prescription dose gave D50 = 48.0 Gy, and grade ≥3 GI toxicity D50 = 87.1 Gy (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis1.pdf). QUANTEC's mean-liver-dose limits (13 Gy/3fx and 18 Gy/6fx for primary liver tumors; 15 Gy/3fx and 20 Gy/6fx for metastases) were judged to remain reasonable, alongside a planning guideline of sparing ≥700 cm³ of liver to ≤15–17 Gy.

**Pancreas — TCP.** Pooling reported dose-fractionation schedules and converting to 3-fraction-equivalent dose, 1-year local control without surgery was ≈79–86% for the equivalent of 30–36 Gy/3fx, with a dose-response across 25–36 Gy and <70% 1-year LC below 24 Gy/3fx (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis2.pdf). The 33 Gy/5fx regimen used in the Alliance A021501 trial converts to 28.2 Gy/3fx, for which the pooled model gives 77% 1-year LC without surgery; above an equivalent 28 Gy/3fx, margin-negative resection was associated with 1-year LC exceeding 90%. The authors caution these findings given wide heterogeneity in local-control definitions and reporting across the pooled studies.

**Adrenal — TCP.** 1-year local control exceeded 95% at BED10 = 116.4 Gy (≈EQD2 97 Gy), achievable with schedules such as 45 Gy/3fx (BED10 = 112.5 Gy) or 55 Gy/5fx (BED10 = 115.5 Gy) (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis3.pdf).

### Pelvic

**Prostate — TCP.** For low/intermediate-risk disease, 5-year freedom-from-biochemical-recurrence reached 90% at EQD2 = 71 Gy and 95% at EQD2 = 90 Gy (α/β = 1.5 Gy assumed); high-risk disease required EQD2 = 97 Gy (90%) / 102 Gy (95%) (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis4.pdf). Most published series use 35–40 Gy in 4–5 fractions; the paper explicitly recommends against exceeding these doses outside of a protocol, since toxicity rises without further demonstrated TCP benefit.

**Prostate — NTCP.** Suggested planning constraints: bladder V(Rx dose) <5–10 cc, urethra Dmax <38–42 Gy, rectum Dmax <35–38 Gy (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Abdomen and Pelvis5.pdf). The authors are explicit that current data do not offer firm tolerance-dose guidance — these are reasonable planning ranges drawn from heterogeneous low-event-rate series, not validated thresholds. Toxicity (mostly grade 1–2) correlated with both dosimetric factors (bladder/urethra/rectum dose-volume) and non-dosimetric patient factors (baseline urinary symptoms, prostate volume, prior TURP).

### Spinal

**Spinal metastases — TCP.** Pooling 2,619 lesions across 24 studies, dose thresholds for 80/90/95% 2-year local control were derived by fraction number — e.g., single fraction: 18 Gy = 82%, 20 Gy = 90%, 22 Gy = 94%, 24 Gy = 96%; 3 fractions: 27 Gy = 78%, 30 Gy = 85% (MP Library/Hytec organ specific-Spinal Cord.pdf). The paper recommends a minimum tumor dose (Dmin) >14–15 Gy (1-fraction-equivalent), or GTV D95 >18.3 Gy, to avoid epidural under-dosage — a reminder that mean/prescription dose alone can mask a clinically important cold spot at the epidural margin, the region most likely to determine local control.

*Spinal cord tolerance itself (the NTCP/myelopathy side of spine SBRT) is summarized in the Overview paper's Table 2 (Dmax by fraction number, 1–5 fx) but the organ-specific NTCP companion paper for spinal cord was not among the files reviewed for this ingest; treat spinal cord Dmax constraints here as (UNVERIFIED) pending a dedicated read of that paper.*

### Mechanism and cross-reference to LQ-model breakdown

The HyTEC biology paper's central finding is that doses above roughly 10 Gy/fraction cause substantial tumor vascular endothelial damage, producing a distinct "indirect" tumor-cell-death pathway (via secondary ischemic death) alongside direct clonogenic sterilization (MP Library/HyTech Biology.pdf). This is offered as one of the leading mechanistic explanations for why the standard linear-quadratic model underestimates SBRT efficacy at high dose per fraction. [[concept_fractionation]] already flags a genuine cross-source discrepancy on exactly where LQ breaks down: the *Radiobiology Self-Assessment Guide* places the threshold below 10 Gy/fraction, while the *Radiobiology Textbook* places it around 18–20 Gy/fraction. The HyTEC biology paper's ~10 Gy/fraction figure sits on the lower-threshold side of that unresolved disagreement — noted here as an additional data point, not a resolution, since [[concept_fractionation]]'s own history explicitly declined to smooth the discrepancy over. See also [[technique_SBRT]] for the vascular-damage and immune-response mechanisms already documented there from a different source.
