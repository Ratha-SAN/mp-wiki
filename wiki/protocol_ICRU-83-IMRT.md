## Synthesis

**What this report is.** ICRU Report 83, *Prescribing, Recording, and Reporting Photon-Beam Intensity-Modulated Radiation Therapy (IMRT)* (2010), extends the [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]/[[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]] framework to IMRT and 3D-CRT, driven by two problems those earlier reports could not anticipate: IMRT's much steeper intra-PTV dose gradients (>10%/mm at plan boundaries) make a single-point dose report unreliable, and the "optimized planning" (inverse planning) process searches an enormous parameter space guided by an objective function whose exact solution path is not fully visible to the user — making rigorous record-keeping essential (MP Library/2010_@medphyslib_ICRU_Committee.pdf).

**Central shift: point dose → dose-volume reporting.** The report's headline recommendation is that IMRT/3D-CRT dose be reported via dose-volume histogram (DVH) metrics rather than the single ICRU Reference Point of ICRU 50. The near-minimum dose D98% (dose covering 98% of a volume) replaces Dmin, and the near-maximum dose D2% replaces Dmax — both are far less sensitive to single-voxel calculation noise than true min/max. The median dose D50% is recommended as always reported (any other clinically relevant DV, such as D95%, may be added but does not replace D98%). A worked example: D98%=57 Gy, D95%=57.5 Gy, D50%=60 Gy, D2%=63 Gy for a single PTV. Mean dose (Dmean) is also always computable from a DVH and equals total energy imparted divided by mass; for a typical near-Gaussian PTV DVH, Dmean ≈ D50%, but this is markedly not true for OARs, where the mean and median can diverge substantially given their usually non-uniform dose distribution — a distinction the report is explicit about.

**Homogeneity and conformity indices.** Homogeneity Index HI = (D2% − D98%) / D50% (0 = ideally homogeneous) replaces older max/min-based indices, since D50% is already the recommended normalization value. Conformity Index CI = Treated Volume ÷ PTV volume (same definition as ICRU 62's Conformity Index — see [[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]]), applicable only when Treated Volume fully encloses the PTV. Dose homogeneity and dose conformity are explicitly independent axes of plan quality — a plan can be low-homogeneity/high-conformity, high-homogeneity/low-conformity, etc., and the choice between them is a genuine planning trade-off (e.g. unequal beam weighting to protect OARs sacrifices homogeneity for conformity).

**Biological evaluation metrics (used cautiously).** The report documents but explicitly cautions against over-reliance on: the logistic TCP model TCP = 1/(1+(d50%/D)^(4γ50%)); the Poisson/clonogen-survival TCP model TCP = exp(−N·SF), generalizable to multi-region tumors as TCP = Π exp(−nᵢ·SF(Dᵢ,dᵢ)) when clonogen density/sensitivity varies spatially within the tumor; the Lyman NTCP power-law volume-effect model TDᵥ = TD(v=1)·v^(−s) (s≈1 for parallel-like organs, s≈0 for serial-like organs — the same qualitative distinction as [[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]]'s serial/parallel classification, now given a quantitative exponent); the critical-element/FSU-based NTCP = 1−[1−p(D)]^N; and Equivalent Uniform Dose (EUD), a single-scalar biologically-weighted dose with a complication-specific exponent a (large positive a for serial-like tissues, negative/large-magnitude a for parallel-like tissues and tumors) — explicitly warned to be sensitive to the choice of a, with the report's own liver-treatment example showing EUD ranking of two competing plans (proton vs. tomotherapy) can flip depending on the assumed a value. Probability of uncomplicated control (PUC) combining TCP and NTCP is presented but flagged as of severely limited practical utility because TCP and NTCP are not statistically independent (a patient more radiosensitive for toxicity may also be more radiosensitive for tumor control).

**Reporting for OARs and the Planning Organ at Risk Volume (PRV).** For parallel-like OARs, more than one dose-volume metric should be reported (Dmean plus VD, e.g. V20Gy for lung, since Dmean and Dmedian diverge and V20Gy correlates with pneumonitis risk). For serial-like OARs, D2% is recommended in place of the older single-point Dmax, since D2% is less sensitive to whether the entire organ or only its high-dose portion was delineated. Because most real organs are neither purely serial nor purely parallel, the report recommends at least three dose-volume specifications be reported when the architecture is ambiguous: Dmean, D2%, and a clinically-correlated VD. PRV is defined identically to [[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]]'s concept (OAR + margin for OAR motion/setup uncertainty, by the same IM/SM logic as the PTV).

**Genuine policy change from ICRU 62 — margins should not be compromised.** ICRU 62 had allowed reducing the PTV or PRV margin as a pragmatic option when they encroach on each other or on an OAR (see [[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]], §2.9 Scenario C). ICRU 83 explicitly reverses this: "it is now recommended that the delineation of the primary PTV margins should not be compromised," citing modern treatment-planning software's ability to achieve adequate OAR sparing through the optimizer's priority rules or by subdividing the PTV into differently-constrained subvolumes (PTVSV) rather than shrinking the anatomically/geometrically-derived margin itself — while still reporting dose for the whole, uncompromised PTV. This is a direct evolution of guidance across the report series, not a contradiction between two contemporaneous sources, but it does mean margin-compromise language in ICRU 62 should be read as superseded by this report for IMRT-era planning.

**New volume: Remaining Volume at Risk (RVR).** RVR = imaged patient volume, minus all delineated CTVs and OARs — i.e., every voxel the plan does not otherwise account for. The report recommends contouring and dose-reporting the RVR because IMRT's larger low/intermediate-dose-bath volumes (see the CI discussion above and the Irradiated-Volume growth pattern already noted in [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]) can otherwise hide unsuspected regions of substantial dose outside any named structure — of particular relevance to second-malignancy risk estimation in long-survival (e.g. pediatric) patients; see [[concept_radiation-carcinogenesis]].

**GTV subtyping and functional imaging.** The report formalizes GTV-T (primary tumor), GTV-N (nodal), GTV-M (distant metastatic) sub-nomenclature and explicit dose/time annotation (e.g. GTV-T(CT, 0 Gy) vs. GTV-T(FDG-PET, 20 Gy) for the same lesion at different imaging modalities/timepoints), motivated by its own clinical example showing FDG-PET, contrast CT, and fat-saturated T2 MRI giving GTV volumes differing by >20% for the same head-and-neck tumor, with FDG-PET showing the most pronounced volume regression under treatment. The report explicitly declines to endorse "biological target volume," "proliferative target volume," or "hypoxic target volume" as distinct named categories, preferring annotated GTV variants instead.

**Dose-calculation accuracy criteria (replacing ICRU 50's single 5% figure).** For low-gradient regions (relative dose change <20%/cm), 85% of target-volume dose samples should be within 5% of the intended dose. For high-gradient regions (≥20%/cm), the report recommends switching from a dose-accuracy criterion to a distance-to-agreement criterion: 85% of dose samples within 5 mm of the intended spatial position. Records (all parameters describing the delivered absorbed-dose distribution) should be retained for the patient's life plus a minimum of 5 years, or per local regulatory requirements — longer for clinical-trial data, retained as long as scientifically needed.
