## Synthesis

**What these documents are.** IAEA Training Course Series TCS-37 (*Clinical Training of Medical Physicists Specializing in Radiation Oncology*, Vienna 2009), TCS-47 (*...Diagnostic Radiology*, Vienna 2010), and TCS-50 (*...Nuclear Medicine*, Vienna 2011) are a three-specialty family of structured clinical-training-programme guides, all produced under the same IAEA/RCA regional technical cooperation project RAS6038 for the Asia-Pacific region. TCS-37 was the original (2009); TCS-47 and TCS-50 explicitly describe themselves as companion publications following the same approach and template. All three were piloted in Thailand, with several other Member States undergoing testing at time of publication.

**The 4-year qualification formula (identical across all three).** All three documents converge on the same clinically-qualified-medical-physicist (CQMP) formula: university bachelor's degree in physics/engineering/equivalent physical science, plus a postgraduate medical physics academic qualification (Master's, typically 18-24 months), plus **at least 2 years (full-time equivalent) structured, supervised in-service clinical training** in a hospital with access to full services in the relevant specialty — **at least 4 years total** post-bachelor's. All three state identically that "the holder of a university degree in medical physics without the required hospital training cannot be considered clinically qualified," and that Member States without a Master's programme with the required content are encouraged to formulate a strategy to achieve one. This is the detailed primary-source elaboration behind the brief "postgraduate degree plus 2-3 years structured clinical training" formula already summarized (from the WHO/IAEA equipment-specifications document) in [[protocol_establishing-radiotherapy-service]] — that page's figure is confirmed accurate and slightly rounded; these three documents are its actual source framework.

**Why the programme exists (identical rationale across all three).** All three cite the same worldwide shortage of clinically qualified medical physicists, most acute in developing countries, driven partly by migration of physics professionals to countries where the profession is better recognized. All three frame the resident as an employee of the training hospital who contributes to routine departmental duties, initially as an assistant, becoming progressively more independent — in the final 6-12 months of training, the resident makes an independent contribution requiring only limited supervision, so the training investment is "repaid" by increased departmental contribution over the training period.

**Identical management and document structure.** All three documents share: (1) the same national-programme-management structure (national responsible authority, national steering committee, national programme coordinator, professional body, support group), plus external pilot-programme coordination; (2) the same six-appendix template — Appendix I Handbook for Residents, Appendix II Handbook for Clinical Supervisors, Appendix III Implementation Guide, Appendix IV Clinical Training Guide (the largest section by far in all three — pp. 39-155 in TCS-37, similarly dominant in TCS-47/50), Appendix V Competency Assessment, Appendix VI Supplementary Forms and Documents.

**Specialty-specific differences (from documents read to date).** The three specialties differ in (a) the specific roles/responsibilities enumerated for the physicist — TCS-37 (radiation oncology) covers dosimetry, treatment planning, quality control, equipment selection/commissioning, and radiation safety in the therapy context; TCS-47 (diagnostic radiology) covers dosimetry, image quality, optimization, radiation safety, QA and equipment management across imaging modalities; TCS-50 (nuclear medicine) covers dosimetry, image quality, optimization, radiation safety, QA/equipment management for unsealed radioactive sources, plus explicit sign-off authority over returning repaired equipment to clinical use; (b) drafting committee membership and publication year — TCS-37 (2009, chaired by J. Drew, Australia; drafting drew on Australian/Croatian/Swedish clinical-training experience); TCS-47 (2010, committee including L. Collins (Australia), J.E. Gray (USA), K.-H. Ng (Malaysia), D. Sutton (UK), B.J. Thomas (Australia); drew on Australia/NZ, UK, and US clinical-training experience); TCS-50 (2011, chaired by R. Fulton (Australia), committee including K. Afroj Quadir (Bangladesh), B. Axelsson (Sweden), T. Bokulic (Croatia), A. Krisanachinda (Thailand), B.J. Thomas (Australia); drew on Australian/Croatian/Swedish experience, same as TCS-37). (UNVERIFIED beyond the introductory/front-matter sections read: the detailed content of Appendix IV Clinical Training Guide and Appendix V Competency Assessment — the largest, most substantive sections of all three documents — has not yet been read for any of the three specialties. This page currently synthesizes only the introductory/rationale/structural material common to all three; the actual module-by-module clinical training content remains to be ingested in a future pass.)
