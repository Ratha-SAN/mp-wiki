## Synthesis

**Scope of this page.** Covers radiation safety equipment for EBRT (Section 3.11) and brachytherapy (Section 4.7) as specified in WHO/IAEA's *Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment* (2021) (MP Library/Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment.pdf) — the personnel- and area-monitoring equipment layer, distinct from the beam-dosimetry equipment covered in [[equipment_dosimetry-and-QC]].

**Survey meters — the common baseline.** Both EBRT and brachytherapy specifications require a portable ionization-chamber-based survey meter (detecting X-ray/gamma radiation above 25 keV, 0.5 µSv/hr–50 mSv/hr range, energy dependence <20% across 50 keV–1 MeV, caesium-137 protection-level calibration every two years) used for comprehensive surveys at equipment installation and periodically thereafter — for brachytherapy specifically, also at every source exchange. A neutron meter (a spherical rem-counter probe, 30 nSv/hr–80 mSv/hr range, calibration traceable to primary standards) is additionally required wherever LINAC photon energy reaches 10 MV or higher — directly following from the neutron-production discussion in [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]]. Given the expense and infrequent use of neutron survey meters, the report suggests departments borrow or share one rather than each purchasing their own — the same national-resource-sharing logic already noted for anthropomorphic end-to-end-testing phantoms in [[equipment_dosimetry-and-QC]].

**Personnel monitoring.** A passive radiation-badge monitoring service (sensitive to X-ray/gamma/beta radiation, exchanged every one to three months, with dose reports forwarded to the radiation regulator if national dose limits are exceeded) is standard for both EBRT and brachytherapy staff. Direct-readout electronic personal dosimeters (audible dose-rate indication, 45 keV–1.2 MeV energy range, accumulated-dose display in Sv) are additionally recommended for situations involving direct radioactive-material handling — source exchanges being the specific example given in both sections, reflecting that passive-badge monitoring alone gives no real-time feedback during exactly the highest-risk handling operations.

**EBRT-specific area monitoring (Table 12).** No dedicated area radiation monitor is specified beyond the survey meter and personal monitoring — consistent with a LINAC or cobalt-60 unit having no radioactive material present when beam-off (LINAC) or a source that is mechanically retracted into a shielded position by default (cobalt-60, covered under [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]]'s own safety section).

**Brachytherapy-specific radiation safety equipment (Table 17) — genuinely more equipment than EBRT.** Because an HDR brachytherapy source is a portable, dischargeable sealed source (rather than a fixed teletherapy source or a beam that switches off), brachytherapy safety equipment is more extensive: a radiation area monitor (audible/visible signal for both treatment room and control panel, usually bundled with the afterloader purchase) providing continuous confirmation the source has returned to its shielded position; an emergency storage container (portable, lead-shielded, with a source-handling tool) for situations where the source cannot mechanically retract into the afterloader — this is the same emergency container already noted as HDR afterloader accessory equipment in [[equipment_HDR-brachytherapy-afterloader]] (Figure 15), cross-referenced here as safety equipment rather than treatment-delivery equipment; and a lead apron (0.5 mm lead equivalent, with thyroid collar and/or a lead-glass console screen) specifically for staff operating the C-arm fluoroscopic X-ray unit during brachytherapy imaging — since, per [[equipment_simulators-and-imaging]], the C-arm operator remains in the treatment room during fluoroscopic exposure rather than retreating to a shielded control room as for other imaging modalities.

**QA.** Both sections specify the same requirement: periodic recalibration of all safety equipment at an SSDL or PSDL with protection-level calibration capability — the same standards-laboratory traceability chain already established for reference dosimetry equipment in [[equipment_dosimetry-and-QC]] and for absolute dose calibration in [[protocol_TRS-398-absorbed-dose-external-beam]], applied here to protection-level (much lower dose-rate range) instruments rather than treatment-level dosimeters.
