## Synthesis

**Why this is a distinct topic from radiotherapy dosimetry.** Everything else in this wiki's dose/dosimetry coverage (concept_radiation-interactions, standard_ICRP-radiation-protection, equipment_radiation-detectors) is framed around therapeutic or general radiation-protection dosimetry. Diagnostic and interventional imaging uses its own standardized dose metrics, distinct from absorbed dose/Gy at the treatment-planning level, because the goal is comparing/regulating scanner and fluoroscope output rather than prescribing a therapeutic dose (raw/Radiobiology Self-Assessment Guide.pdf, Yu & Abazeed, eds., 2016, Ch.20).

**CT dosimetry.** CT dose index (CTDI) is the internationally standardized (IEC 60601-2-44) CT dose metric, measured in one of two cylindrical PMMA phantoms: 32 cm ("body") or 16 cm ("head," used for both adult and paediatric head studies — note paediatric torso scans are, somewhat awkwardly, referenced to the 32 cm body phantom rather than a paediatric-sized one). CTDI₁₀₀ is measured with a 100 mm ion chamber at both central and peripheral phantom positions; CTDIw is the weighted average (1/3 central + 2/3 peripheral). CTDIvol further scales CTDIw by pitch to give a volume-averaged value — this is the metric all commercial CT scanners must display per scan and log in the radiation dose structured report. Critically, **CTDIvol is not equal to patient dose** — it is the dose to a standard cylindrical phantom, not the specific patient; patient-specific dose requires further adjustment for patient size/tube potential/current. Dose-length product (DLP) = CTDIvol × scan length, proportional to total energy deposited; DLP converts to an effective-dose estimate (mSv) via an age-dependent k-factor (e.g. adult head CT k=0.0021 mSv/(mGy·cm) vs. newborn k=0.011 — a >5-fold difference reflecting paediatric organ sensitivity, consistent with the age-dependent radiosensitivity already covered in [[concept_age-and-sex-radiosensitivity]]). CTDIvol from a head phantom is known to overestimate actual eye-lens dose compared to Monte Carlo simulation with realistic patient models — relevant to the lens-dose limits in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]] and to [[concept_cataract-and-cardiovascular-late-effects]].

**Fluoroscopy/interventional dosimetry.** Regulatory dose-rate limits (US) apply only in live fluoroscopy mode: 88 mGy/min normal, 176 mGy/min high-dose mode under defined geometry — acquisition (image-recording) mode has no regulatory dose-rate limit, only protocol/hardware limits. Automatic dose-rate control (ADRC) continuously adjusts kVp/mA/pulse width/filtration to hold constant detector dose for consistent image quality, at the lowest achievable skin dose rate. Spectral (copper) filtration hardens the beam, preferentially removing low-energy photons to reduce skin entrance dose per unit detector dose. Magnification trades off directly against dose rate: for older image-intensifier systems, dose rate scales with the inverse square of the field-of-view ratio (e.g. 9-inch→6-inch FOV increases dose rate by (9/6)² = 2.25×); digital detectors show a less predictable but still generally increasing relationship. Dose metrics used clinically: cumulative air kerma (Ka,r, at the interventional reference point) and air kerma-area product (AKAP) — fluoroscopes made after 2012 must report one of these. Neither directly equals skin entrance dose (Ka,r differs by a mass-attenuation-coefficient ratio; AKAP is even further removed, being distance-independent), though AKAP correlates better with effective dose. **Deterministic skin injury from diagnostic-energy fluoroscopy is a real, if rare, clinical risk** — prolonged interventional procedures (cardiac catheterization, vascular surgery, electrophysiology) can deliver skin doses exceeding thresholds for erythema, epilation, desquamation, or even necrosis, despite using "diagnostic" energy x-rays — a direct real-world instance of the deterministic-effect thresholds already tabulated in [[concept_whole-body-irradiation]], now in a non-radiotherapy clinical context.
