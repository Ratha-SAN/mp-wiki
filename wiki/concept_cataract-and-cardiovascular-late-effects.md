## Synthesis

**Radiation-induced cataract.** Cataract (lens opacification) is the leading cause of blindness worldwide; radiation cataract specifically develops from damage to lens epithelial cells in the germinative zone, which normally differentiate into lens fiber cells (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.8 §8.2.3.1). ICRP/radiation-protection practice currently classifies radiation cataract as a tissue reaction (deterministic effect) with an assumed threshold — already noted at ~0.5 Gy in [[concept_whole-body-irradiation]] — but this source frames it more precisely as a "cataractogenic load" concept: chronic low-dose exposure adds to a lifetime accumulation of combined genetic and environmental cataract-inducing factors, and whether an individual actually develops a clinically significant cataract depends on where they land relative to that cumulative threshold, not on radiation dose in isolation. This is a somewhat softer framing than a hard deterministic threshold, though the source does not explicitly reclassify cataract as stochastic (Ch.8 §8.2.3.1).

**Radiation-induced cardiovascular disease (CVD) — a comparatively newly recognized late effect.** Ionizing radiation is now understood to contribute to atherosclerosis-mediated diseases (ischaemic heart disease, cerebrovascular disease, myocardial infarction, stroke) with a long latency (10–15 years) and a proposed deterministic threshold around 0.5 Gy — though the source notes the weight of evidence for including this in radiation protection frameworks has only accumulated within roughly the last decade (Ch.8 §8.2.3.2). Most evidence comes from thoracic radiotherapy cohorts (breast cancer, Hodgkin lymphoma, lung cancer) rather than occupational/environmental cohorts. A concrete clinical example: left-sided breast cancer patients historically received ~6.6 Gy mean cumulative heart dose vs. ~2.9 Gy for right-sided — heart-sparing techniques (deep inspiration breath hold, prone positioning, target-specific delivery) have reduced mean heart dose from ~4.6 Gy (2014) to ~2.6 Gy (2017) across 99 studies, though doses remain clinically non-trivial and late cardiotoxicity continues to occur (Ch.8 §8.2.3.2). Mechanistically, high-dose cardiotoxicity is reasonably well characterized (oxidative stress, DNA damage, endothelial adhesion molecule upregulation — consistent with mechanisms already in [[concept_radiation-sources]] and [[concept_normal-tissue-response]]), but low-dose mechanisms, dose-rate dependence, and epigenetic contributions remain poorly studied — an "adverse outcome pathway" framework has been proposed to organize the mechanistic evidence for predictive/population-level use. Notably, the most recent epidemiological evidence describes increasing *probability* (not severity) of cardiovascular effects with dose — the classic signature of a stochastic effect — creating tension with the "deterministic effect with 0.5 Gy threshold" classification also stated in the same source (Ch.8 §8.2.3.2) (flagged here as an internal tension in the source's own framing, not a contradiction with other wiki pages).
