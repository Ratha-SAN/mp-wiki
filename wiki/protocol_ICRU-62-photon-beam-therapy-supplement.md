## Synthesis

**What this report is.** ICRU Report 62, *Prescribing, Recording and Reporting Photon Beam Therapy (Supplement to ICRU Report 50)* (1999), does not revise the GTV/CTV/PTV/Treated/Irradiated Volume/OAR definitions of [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]] — it explicitly states those oncological concepts "need not be reconsidered" — but replaces ICRU 50's single-step PTV margin with a two-part decomposition, introduces the Planning Organ at Risk Volume (PRV), a Conformity Index, and a serial/parallel functional classification for organ tolerance (MP Library/1999_@medphyslib_ICRU_Commitee_Prescribing.pdf). The motivation given is the rise of conformal therapy: tighter margins improve therapeutic gain but increase the clinical cost of a marginal geometric miss, which made the composition of the PTV margin worth separating into its physiologically distinct sources of uncertainty.

**Internal Margin (IM) and Internal Target Volume (ITV).** The Internal Margin compensates specifically for physiological variation in the CTV's size, shape, and position relative to a fixed internal reference point (e.g. respiration, bladder/rectal filling, peristalsis, cardiac motion) — sources that are difficult or impossible to control by better setup. CTV + IM = the Internal Target Volume (ITV), a new named volume not present in ICRU 50.

**Set-up Margin (SM).** A separate margin compensates for technical/setup uncertainty in patient-beam positioning: patient positioning variation, mechanical uncertainty of the treatment machine (gantry/collimator/couch sag), CT-to-treatment-unit transfer errors, and human factors — sources that, unlike the IM, can be reduced by better immobilization, imaging, and machine QA. PTV = ITV + SM (i.e. CTV + combined IM and SM), and the two margins are explicitly not summed linearly to the PTV — the report recommends a quadratic-combination approach (analogous to combining independent uncertainties in quadrature) to avoid an excessively (and clinically unusable) large PTV, while noting this ideal is strictly achievable only where all uncertainties and their standard deviations are actually known.

**Conformity Index (CI).** Defined as Treated Volume ÷ PTV volume, applicable only when the Treated Volume fully encloses the PTV — a single number quantifying how tightly a plan's high-dose region matches the target relative to the older, purely descriptive Treated/Irradiated Volume ratios of ICRU 50. The report's own worked prostate example shows CI dropping (improving) as beam number increases (2 opposed beams → CI≈4.35, 4 intersecting beams → CI≈2.61, arc therapy → CI≈2.18) and improving further with multileaf-collimator shaping of 3–4 beams (CI≈1.74–1.60) — a concrete illustration that adding beams and conformal blocking both reduce Treated Volume relative to PTV, though the Irradiated Volume (50% isodose, low-dose bath) simultaneously grows with more beam directions (from 8.56× PTV at 2 beams up to ~11.9× PTV at 3–4 intersecting beams before MLC blocking pulls it back down).

**Organs at Risk: serial/parallel/serial-parallel classification and the Planning Organ at Risk Volume (PRV).** Building on the Functional Subunit (FSU) concept, an organ's tolerance behavior is classified by its functional architecture: serial structures (e.g. spinal cord) can lose function from damage to even a small subvolume — "relative seriality" is high; parallel structures (e.g. lung) tolerate damage roughly in proportion to the fraction of organ volume exceeding tolerance dose; some organs are serial-parallel or mixed (e.g. the heart — combined serial coronary arteries and parallel myocardium; a nephron). Analogous to the PTV, a margin is added around the OR for its own motion/setup uncertainty, giving the Planning Organ at Risk Volume (PRV) — and the report notes PTV and PRV may, and often do, overlap, requiring a compromise between adequate CTV coverage and OAR sparing. This qualitative serial/parallel classification is the same distinction later formalized quantitatively via the LKB effective-volume exponent n in [[concept_normal-tissue-response]] (n≈0 for serial organs, n≈1 for parallel organs) — ICRU 62 supplies the underlying organ-architecture rationale for that later quantitative model.

**Reporting doses across a series of patients.** ICRU 50 addressed reporting for a single patient; ICRU 62 adds recommendations for protocols/trials: describe the prescribed GTV/CTV/PTV definitions and dose/fractionation per study arm (worked example given: a prostate dose-escalation trial with three GTV/CTV/PTV definition groups), and report, for the treated series, the proportion of patients whose delivered dose varied from the prescribed ICRU Reference Point dose by <±5%, ±5–10%, and >±10% — since real-world delivery (interruptions, hardware issues, re-evaluation to palliative intent) routinely deviates from a trial's nominal prescription in ways not visible from the protocol document alone.

**Color convention.** The report also recommends a fixed color code — GTV dark red, CTV light red, ITV dark blue, PTV light blue, OR dark green, PRV light green, landmarks black — for consistent graphical reporting across centers; a minor but concrete interoperability recommendation.
