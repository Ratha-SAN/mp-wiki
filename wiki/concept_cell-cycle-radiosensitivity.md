## Synthesis

**Cell cycle phases and control.** The cycle is G1 → S (DNA synthesis) → G2 → M, with quiescent cells in G0; typical cycle time 10–40 h (G1 ~30%, S ~50%, G2 ~15%, M ~5%) (raw/Radiation Biology- A Handbook for Teachers and Students.pdf, §2.3.7, §3.4). Progression is driven by cyclin–cyclin-dependent-kinase (Cdk) complexes: cyclin D–Cdk4/6 and cyclin E–Cdk2 drive the G1/S transition (via Rb phosphorylation, releasing E2F), cyclin A–Cdk2 drives S-phase, cyclin B–Cdk1 drives G2/M (§3.4.1–3.4.2). Cdk inhibitors (KIP family: p21, p27, p57; INK4 family: p15/p16/p18/p19) enforce arrest; p21 is transcriptionally induced by p53 after DNA damage (§3.4.3).

**Radiosensitivity varies through the cycle.** Late S-phase is generally most radioresistant (attributed to dominance of high-fidelity HR repair); G2/M is most radiosensitive (chromatin compaction limits repair enzyme access); G1 is intermediate (§2.3.7). This pattern is not universal — some oncogene-transformed or DNA-repair-deficient cells show flat sensitivity across the cycle (§3.9).

**Checkpoints.** DNA integrity checkpoints (G1/S, intra-S, G2/M) delay cycle progression to allow repair before replication or mitosis; radiation induces ~3–4 h of G2 delay per Gy in diploid cells (§3.9). The G1 checkpoint is ATM→CHK→p53→p21→cyclinE-Cdk2 dependent; it is abrogated in p53-mutant or ATM/Rb-deficient cells (most tumours), which nonetheless typically retain a functional G2 checkpoint — see [[concept_dna-damage-and-repair]] for the signalling cascade. Checkpoint-abrogating drugs (caffeine, UCN-01, methylxanthines) force premature mitosis and mitotic catastrophe, preferentially sensitizing p53-mutant tumour cells (§3.9).

**Redistribution/recruitment in fractionated therapy.** Cells surviving one fraction are non-randomly distributed across the cycle (enriched in resistant phases); subsequent progression into sensitive phases plus recruitment of quiescent G0 stem cells into cycle both tend to increase sensitivity to the next fraction — this is the "redistribution" R of the five Rs of fractionation (§3.12.3; see [[concept_fractionation]]).

**Oncogene/tumour-suppressor effects on cell cycle and radioresponse.** p53 mutation (>50% of human cancers) abolishes G1 arrest and correlates with acquired radioresistance; RAS activation, RAF/MEK/ERK and PI3K/Akt pathway activation are also linked to radioresistance and are targets for radiosensitization strategies (farnesyl transferase inhibitors, PI3K inhibitors, EGFR inhibitors) (§3.3.2–3.3.3, §3.9).
