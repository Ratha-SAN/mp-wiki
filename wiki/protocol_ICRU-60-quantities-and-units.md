## Synthesis

**What this report is.** ICRU Report 60, *Fundamental Quantities and Units for Ionizing Radiation* (issued 30 December 1998), is the formal-definitions backbone underlying every dose, fluence, and interaction-coefficient quantity used elsewhere in this wiki's ICRU series (MP Library/1998_@medphyslib_ICRU_Committee.pdf). It supersedes Part A of ICRU Report 33 (1980) for general-use quantities/units; radiation-protection-specific quantities/units were already split off separately into ICRU Report 51 (1993). This is a short (24-page), dense reference report — read here in full — rather than a clinical protocol; it defines *what a quantity means*, not how to use it clinically.

**Radiometry — describing the radiation field itself, before any interaction with matter.** Quantities are defined as successive derivatives of two base quantities: particle number N (count) and radiant energy R (=NE for particles of energy E). Fluence Φ = dN/da (particles crossing a unit cross-sectional sphere) and energy fluence Ψ = dR/da are the field-description quantities used throughout dosimetric calculations (e.g. kerma is directly computed from fluence — see below). Fluence rate (Φ̇) and energy fluence rate (Ψ̇) add a time derivative; particle/energy radiance (Φ̇Ω, Ψ̇Ω) add a directional (solid-angle) derivative. Each scalar radiometric quantity has a vectorial counterpart (vectorial fluence rate = current density in transport-theory terms) used when direction matters, e.g. in Monte Carlo transport calculations of the kind referenced in [[protocol_ICRU-48-phantoms]] and [[protocol_ICRU-56-beta-ray-dosimetry]].

**Interaction coefficients — how radiation interacts with a specific material.** The cross section σ (unit: m², commonly expressed in barns, 1 b = 10⁻²⁸ m²) is the fundamental interaction coefficient, from which all others are derived: mass attenuation coefficient μ/ρ (probability of any interaction per unit mass thickness), mass energy transfer coefficient μtr/ρ (fraction of energy transferred to charged-particle kinetic energy), mass energy absorption coefficient μen/ρ = (μtr/ρ)(1−g) where g is the fraction subsequently lost to radiative processes (bremsstrahlung) — this is the coefficient underlying kerma-to-dose approximations discussed below, and the same coefficient whose tissue/tissue-substitute matching is the subject of [[protocol_ICRU-44-tissue-substitutes]]. Mass stopping power S/ρ (energy lost by a charged particle per unit path length, per unit density) splits into three independent components — electronic/collisional (Sel, dominant at therapeutic energies), radiative (Srad, bremsstrahlung — dominant only at very high electron energy or high-Z materials), and nuclear (Snuc, elastic Coulomb recoil, generally negligible at radiotherapy energies). Linear energy transfer (LET, symbol LΔ) is a *restricted* form of collision stopping power — it excludes the kinetic energy carried away by secondary electrons (δ-rays) above a specified energy cutoff Δ, representing energy considered "locally" deposited along the primary particle's track rather than transported elsewhere by energetic secondaries; this Δ-dependent restriction is why LET values in the literature are not comparable unless the cutoff energy is stated, a a subtlety relevant to any RBE/LET-weighted dose discussion (see [[concept_RBE]]).

**Dosimetry — energy conversion, then energy deposition.** The report explicitly separates dosimetric quantities into two conceptual stages. *Conversion of energy* (transfer from the primary uncharged-particle field to secondary charged particles): kerma K = dEtr/dm (initial kinetic energy of all charged particles liberated by uncharged particles per unit mass — the quantity computed directly from fluence via K = ΦEμtr/ρ) and the newly-introduced (in this report) quantity cema C = dEc/dm, which parallels kerma but for an incident charged-particle field rather than an uncharged one — cema is the energy lost by incoming charged particles in electronic collisions, as opposed to kerma's energy imparted to outgoing charged particles. Exposure X = dQ/dm (ionization charge produced per unit mass of air) remains the air-specific, photon-specific legacy quantity, related to air kerma via X ≈ (e/W)K(1−ḡ) using the W-value already defined in [[protocol_ICRU-31-W-value]]. *Deposition of energy* (where that transferred energy actually ends up): energy deposit εi (energy deposited in a single interaction — the fundamental stochastic quantity all others are built from), energy imparted ε (sum of energy deposits in a volume from all events), lineal energy y = εs/l̄ (energy imparted per unit mean chord length, from a single event — a microdosimetric quantity), specific energy z = ε/m (energy imparted per unit mass — the stochastic quantity whose mean, in the small-domain limit, equals absorbed dose), and finally absorbed dose D = dε̄/dm, the mean energy imparted per unit mass — the single most important dosimetric quantity in this entire wiki, here given its most rigorous formal definition. The report is explicit that kerma equals absorbed dose only under charged-particle equilibrium (CPE) — a condition explicitly named but only loosely invoked in most of this wiki's clinical pages (e.g. [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]'s reference-dose discussion implicitly assumes CPE holds at the measurement point without stating so).

**Radioactivity.** Decay constant λ = dP/dt (probability of spontaneous nuclear transformation per unit time for a single nucleus), half-life T½ = (ln2)/λ, activity A = λN (transformations per unit time for a quantity of radionuclide, unit becquerel), and the air kerma-rate constant Γδ (air kerma rate at unit distance per unit activity, for photons above a stated energy cutoff δ) — the last of these is the practical constant underlying brachytherapy source-strength specification of the kind discussed in [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]] (TRAK) and [[protocol_ICRU-89-cervix-brachytherapy]].

**Why this report matters as a cross-cutting reference.** Nearly every other ICRU report in this wiki uses these quantities without re-deriving them — [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]'s "dose," [[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]]'s D2%/D98% dose-volume metrics, [[protocol_ICRU-91-stereotactic]]'s near-max/near-min dose points, [[protocol_ICRU-56-beta-ray-dosimetry]]'s personal dose equivalent, and [[protocol_HyTEC-dose-constraints]]'s BED/EQD2 figures are all built on the absorbed-dose definition formalized here. This page functions as the wiki's canonical glossary/reference point for those terms rather than a clinical protocol in its own right.
