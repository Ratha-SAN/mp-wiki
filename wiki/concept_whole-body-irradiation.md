## Synthesis

**Deterministic radiation syndromes.** Acute whole-body exposure produces dose- and latency-dependent syndromes, historically divided by dominant critical-tissue failure (raw/Radiation Biology- A Handbook for Teachers and Students.pdf, §2.5.6, §3.11.3, §4.3.2): prodromal syndrome (nausea/vomiting within hours, >~2 Gy); haematopoietic syndrome (2–8 Gy in humans, death at 12–30 days from agranulocytosis/sepsis and thrombocytopenia/haemorrhage; human LD50/30 without bone marrow transplant estimated at 4–7 Gy); gastrointestinal syndrome (~8–15 Gy, death ~1 week from mucosal denudation, infection, fluid/electrolyte loss); neurovascular/cerebrovascular syndrome (>20 Gy, death hours–days, cerebral oedema and cardiovascular collapse) (§4.3.1–4.3.2). Note a cross-source discrepancy on this threshold: "raw/Radiobiology Self-Assessment Guide.pdf" (Yu & Abazeed, eds., 2016, Ch.15, citing Hall & Giaccia) gives the cerebrovascular/CNS syndrome threshold as >100 Gy (death in 24–48 hours) rather than >20 Gy — a 5-fold difference on a specific named threshold. Flagged rather than silently resolved; by contrast, the two sources' gastrointestinal-syndrome dose ranges (5–12 Gy vs. ~8–15 Gy here) overlap closely enough not to be a real discrepancy. Mouse LD50/30 ≈ 7 Gy; LD50 tends to decrease with increasing body mass across species (§4.3.1). A UK Medical Research Council committee replaced the single-LD50 concept for humans with three prognostic dose bands: ≤2 Gy (survival very likely), 2–8 Gy (survival possible with adequate medical care), ≥8 Gy (survival unlikely) (§4.3.1). A more granular threshold/ED50 table (various sources including ICRP 2007), consistent with the ranges above, gives: bone marrow syndrome threshold/LD50 1.5/3.0 Gy (first aid only) vs. 2.2/4.5 Gy (supportive treatment); pneumonitis threshold/LD50 5.5/10.0 Gy; gut syndrome threshold/LD50 10.0/15.0 Gy; and for morbidity endpoints — vomiting ED50 2 Gy (threshold 0.5 Gy), skin burns ED50 20 Gy (threshold 8.6 Gy), cataract ED50 3 Gy (threshold 1.3 Gy; see [[concept_cataract-and-cardiovascular-late-effects]] for the fuller "cataractogenic load" framing and radiation-induced cardiovascular disease as a related late effect), male/female temporary sterility ED50 0.7/3.5 Gy (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.2 §2.7.2.4, Tables 2.9–2.10; see [[concept_radiobiology-history]] source). No contradiction with the ranges already cited above.

**METREPOL system.** Modern clinical management (post-1986) uses the METREPOL framework, grading four organ systems independently — Neurovascular (N), Haematopoietic (H), Cutaneous (C), Gastrointestinal (G) — each on a 1–4 severity scale from mild (spontaneous recovery certain) to fatal, since real accident victims rarely present with the "clean" single-syndrome picture of the classical mouse-derived model (§4.3.2–4.3.3). Haematopoietic grading (H1–H4) is based on lymphocyte/granulocyte/platelet kinetics; H4 (lymphocytes <250/μl by day 2, granulocytes <500/μl by week 1, platelets to 0 by day 10) requires stem-cell transplantation as the only option (§4.3.3.2). The 1999 Tokai-Mura criticality accident introduced the concept of "multi-organ involvement" at very high, inhomogeneous doses — one H4 patient died at 210 days despite transient haematopoietic restoration, from immune failure and reactivated infection (§4.3.3, §4.4.4).

**Triage.** In mass-casualty accidents (e.g. Chernobyl), triage relies on early clinical signs — time to vomiting, day-3 lymphocyte count, hair loss within 2 weeks — rather than time-consuming dose reconstruction; this table was validated after Chernobyl and correlated well with cytogenetic dose and lethality (§2.6.1, §4.3.4, Table 4.1). See [[concept_dna-damage-and-repair]] for the dicentric-chromosome biodosimetry method used for retrospective dose confirmation.

**Medical management** is largely symptomatic and follows medical-oncology principles for cytotoxic-drug-induced marrow toxicity: infection prophylaxis, fluid/electrolyte replacement, platelet transfusion, G-CSF for haematopoietic stimulation, and stem-cell transplantation reserved for severe marrow aplasia with adequate HLA matching (§2.6.1, §4.3.3.2).
