## Synthesis

**Governing bodies.** UNSCEAR reviews and publishes the scientific evidence base; ICRP (an independent charity) issues policy recommendations derived from that evidence; IAEA translates ICRP recommendations into regulatory-style Safety Standards adopted by member states (raw/Radiation Biology- A Handbook for Teachers and Students.pdf, §4.7). Formation history: ICRP was established in 1928, followed by UNSCEAR and the US National Academy of Sciences BEIR (Biological Effects of Ionizing Radiation) committee, both in 1955, and IAEA in 1957 (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Preface; see [[concept_radiobiology-history]]).

**Three principles of radiation protection (ICRP).** Justification (any action altering exposure should do more good than harm), optimisation (ALARA — exposures kept as low as reasonably achievable, economic/societal factors considered; applies to all exposure situations), and dose limitation (a "safety net" applying only to planned exposure situations, excluding medical exposure of patients) (§4.7). See [[concept_ethics-and-risk-communication-in-radiation-protection]] for the underlying ethical framework (ICRP 138: beneficence/non-maleficence, justice, dignity, prudence) and the risk-perception/communication research this system has to operate within in practice.

**Effects classification.** Deterministic effects ("tissue reactions") have a dose threshold and increasing severity with dose (e.g. the [[concept_whole-body-irradiation|acute radiation syndromes]]); stochastic effects (cancer, heritable disease) have no assumed threshold, with probability (not severity) increasing with dose — this is the basis of the linear-non-threshold (LNT) model, which ICRP treats as the best practical basis for protection below 100 mSv/year despite acknowledged scientific uncertainty at very low doses (§4.7.1; see [[concept_radiation-carcinogenesis]]).

**Dose quantities.** Equivalent dose (Sv) = absorbed dose (Gy) × radiation weighting factor (wR; photons/electrons = 1, protons = 2, α particles/heavy ions = 20, neutrons energy-dependent 2.5–20) (§4.7.1, Table 4.3). Note: Baatout (ed.), *Radiobiology Textbook* (2023, Ch.2 Table 2.6), citing the same ICRP 103, gives the neutron wR range as 5–20 rather than 2.5–20 — a minor discrepancy between two secondary summaries of the same primary source rather than a substantive contradiction; ICRP 103's actual neutron wR is a continuous energy-dependent curve, and 2.5 is the value at its low- and high-energy tails, which a coarser table can round away. Neither source here is the primary ICRP 103 publication (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.2 §2.5.3.1). Effective dose (Sv) = Σ(equivalent dose × tissue weighting factor, wT). ICRP 2007 tissue weighting factors: 0.12 each for bone marrow, colon, lung, stomach, breast, and "remainder tissues" (13 organs, summed then weighted 0.12); 0.08 gonads; 0.04 bladder/oesophagus/liver/thyroid; 0.01 bone surface/skin/salivary glands/brain (§4.7.1, Table 4.5) — note breast weighting rose from 0.05 (1990) to 0.12 (2007) and gonad weighting fell from 0.20 to 0.08, reflecting revised risk estimates, especially the large downward revision of heritable-effect risk (see [[concept_heritable-and-embryo-effects]]).

**Nominal risk coefficients (ICRP 2007, detriment-adjusted).** Cancer: 5.5%/Sv (whole population), 4.1%/Sv (adult workers). Hereditary effects: 0.2%/Sv (whole population), 0.1%/Sv (workers) — a 6–8-fold reduction from 1990 values. Combined nominal fatal risk coefficient remains ~5%/Sv, consistent with the 1990 recommendations despite the reweighting (§4.7.1, Table 4.4).

**Dose limits for planned exposure situations (ICRP 2007, excluding medical exposure of patients).**

| Limit | Occupational | Public |
|---|---|---|
| Effective dose | 20 mSv/yr (averaged over 5 yr, ≤50 mSv in any single year) | 1 mSv/yr (averaged over 5 yr) |
| Equivalent dose, lens of eye | 150 mSv/yr (under revision) | 15 mSv/yr |
| Equivalent dose, skin | 500 mSv/yr | 50 mSv/yr |
| Equivalent dose, hands/feet | 500 mSv/yr | — |

Pregnant radiation workers: exposure for the remainder of pregnancy after notification must not exceed 1 mSv, since the embryo/fetus is treated as a member of the public (§4.7.2, Table 4.6). Note: ICRP occupational dose limits do not differentiate by biological sex, despite A-bomb LSS epidemiological data showing roughly 2× higher excess relative risk of solid cancer in women than men (see [[concept_age-and-sex-radiosensitivity]]) — flagged by radiation-protection researchers (MELODI, EURAMED initiatives) as an open research/policy question rather than a settled position (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.7 §7.7.6).

**Mammography screening — a worked risk-benefit case.** Illustrates why effective dose/risk coefficients are for population-level planning, not individual retrospective risk assessment (ICRP 2007, §157, quoted directly in source) (§4.7.1, §4.7.3). Single mammographic exposure ≈4 mGy to breast tissue; LSS and medically-exposed cohort data (TB fluoroscopy, post-partum mastitis series) show a linear dose-response for radiation-induced breast cancer, strongly age-at-exposure dependent, becoming statistically insignificant for exposure after age 50 (§4.7.3). Because both radiogenic risk and false-positive/false-negative rates are less favourable in younger women, and background incidence is much higher after 50, screening is recommended from age 50; benefit:risk ratios in that group are estimated at 20:1 to >100:1, with a >20% mortality reduction demonstrated in large studies (e.g. Sweden) (§4.7.3).
