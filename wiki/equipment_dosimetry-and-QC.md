## Synthesis

**Scope of this page.** Covers dosimetry and quality control equipment for EBRT (Section 3.10) and brachytherapy (Section 4.6) as specified in WHO/IAEA's *Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment* (2021) (MP Library/Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment.pdf). This is the measurement-hardware layer underlying the reference dosimetry protocols in [[protocol_TRS-398-absorbed-dose-external-beam]] and [[protocol_TRS-277-absorbed-dose-photon-electron]].

**Four functionally distinct dosimetry equipment categories (EBRT).** The report draws a clear four-way distinction: *reference dosimetry equipment* establishes and maintains beam calibration under IAEA TRS-398 reference conditions (Table 7: a Farmer-type chamber, a plane-parallel chamber for electron beams, triaxial cables, reference- and field-class electrometers per IEC 60731, a water phantom, and a strontium-90 check source for chamber constancy checks between full calibrations); *relative dosimetry equipment* characterizes beams under non-reference conditions and in 3D (Table 8: a full 3D scanning water tank system for LINAC commissioning, a 2D detector array for dynamic wedge/IMRT commissioning and ongoing QA, radiochromic film with a flatbed scanner and analysis software, solid-water phantom plates as a practical water-tank substitute for routine QC); *quality control equipment* tests the imaging/optical/mechanical performance of treatment and simulation equipment rather than measuring dose directly (Table 9: LINAC alignment cube phantom, laser alignment phantom, CT electron-density calibration phantom, EPID and kV image-quality phantoms); and *in vivo dosimetry equipment* confirms actual delivered dose to the patient during treatment (Table 10: diode entrance-dose detectors for photon/electron beams, a surface detector, an out-of-field detector for peripheral-dose estimation, with a note that competing technologies — MOSFET, TLD, RPLD, OSLD, film — could substitute for the diode system specified, diodes being favoured here for instantaneous readout and reusability). A fifth category, *radiology dosimetry equipment* (Table 11: kV ionization chamber, kVp meter, aluminium filter set, CT pencil chamber, CTDI phantom), is specifically for quantifying the imaging dose from simulation/verification X-rays — distinct from and much lower than the prescribed treatment dose — and the report explicitly flags that a medical physicist trained only in radiation-oncology dosimetry may lack the skills to interpret radiology dosimetry/image-quality results, recommending consultation with a diagnostic-radiology-qualified medical physicist where needed.

**End-to-end testing.** The report frames component-level QA (simulator alone, TPS alone, treatment unit alone) as necessary but not sufficient — recommending, in addition, whole-pathway end-to-end tests using an anthropomorphic phantom carried through simulation, planning, and delivery, with embedded detectors confirming the measured delivered dose matches the planned dose. IAEA has published dedicated end-to-end methodologies for 3D-CRT and IMRT. Given the high cost of anthropomorphic phantoms (available for head-and-neck, thorax, pelvis, and paediatric regions) relative to their low frequency of use, the report suggests departments consider sharing such phantoms at a national level — a resource-pooling recommendation specific to the LMIC context this publication targets.

**EPID as an emerging QC tool.** The report notes a growing trend of using the LINAC's own electronic portal imaging device (already specified in [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]]) as a dosimetric QC tool — patient-specific IMRT QA, MLC performance testing, field-size verification — replacing consumable radiochromic film for these checks, contingent on careful dosimetric commissioning of the EPID itself (plus the licences/calibration/image-access infrastructure this requires).

**Brachytherapy dosimetry and QC (Section 4.6).** Built around the well-type ionization chamber, described as the preferred detector for establishing the reference air kerma rate (RAKR) of the HDR source — recalibration at an SSDL/PSDL recommended every two years. Table 16 otherwise closely mirrors the EBRT reference dosimetry equipment list (triaxial cable, electrometer, thermometer, barometer, caesium-137 check source in place of strontium-90) plus two brachytherapy-specific items: self-developing radiochromic film for source dosimetry, and a source-position check tool (a device supplied by the afterloader vendor, with a cavity accepting radiochromic film for autoradiographic confirmation that the source stops at its programmed dwell positions) — this device addresses a distinct failure mode from external-beam QC (source positioning accuracy inside the patient/applicator) with no EBRT equivalent. The radiology dosimetry equipment and image-quality phantom already specified for EBRT (Table 11) does double duty for C-arm fluoroscopic unit QC in brachytherapy — an explicit equipment-sharing point the report makes to avoid duplicate procurement (see [[equipment_simulators-and-imaging]]).
