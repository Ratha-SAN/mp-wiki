## Synthesis

**Microbeams (research tool).** Photon or particle beams ~1–10 μm in size, deliverable as a single beam or an array, used to irradiate individual cells (or subcellular structures) with precisely controlled dose and localization (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.4 §4.4.1.1). Charged-particle microbeams can deliver exactly one particle per cell, reproducing the low-dose environmental-exposure scenario of radon alpha-particle traversal, and have been central to bystander-effect research (co-cultured glioma/fibroblast experiments implicating nitric oxide and ROS in bystander signalling — see [[concept_non-targeted-and-low-dose-effects]]), to studying subcellular radiosensitivity (e.g. DNA near the nuclear membrane), and to adaptive-response studies (priming doses <0.2 Gy reducing subsequent high-dose effect). One influential early finding (Miller et al., RARAF facility): single alpha-particle traversal did not produce a statistically different transformation frequency than zero traversals — cited as supporting a threshold hypothesis for radiation risk (Ch.4 §4.4.1.1) (relevant context for, though not dispositive of, the LNT discussion in [[concept_radiation-carcinogenesis]]).

**Minibeams and clinical spatial fractionation.** Minibeams (~100 μm–1 mm) are the basis of an emerging clinical approach: microbeam radiation therapy (MRT, photons, ~100 μm beam) and minibeam radiation therapy (MBRT/pMBRT, protons/ions, up to ~1 mm), which spatially fractionate the beam into peaks and valleys (or, for protons/light ions, can achieve homogeneous target dose) to spare healthy tissue in the beam path (Ch.4 §4.4.1.2). The underlying mechanism is the dose-volume effect: smaller irradiated volumes are tolerated to higher doses, attributed to undamaged migratory cells from the spared valleys infiltrating and repairing the damaged peak regions, plus rapid (hours-to-days) repair of capillaries in submillimeter fields supporting surrounding tissue recovery — mechanisms not yet fully understood. Already used clinically in a related, more established form as spatially fractionated radiotherapy (GRID therapy); MRT/MBRT proper are progressing toward clinical trials (Ch.4 §4.4.1.2).
