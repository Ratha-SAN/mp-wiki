## Synthesis

**What this page covers.** The three successive editions of the ICRP's core general recommendations — Publication 26 (1977), Publication 60 (1990), and Publication 103 (2007, current) — read from the primary documents themselves, tracing how the system now summarized in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]] actually evolved. This is a genuine three-generation lineage, not three independent sources: each publication explicitly supersedes its predecessor while stating what it keeps unchanged, which is itself informative about which parts of the system are considered settled versus revisable.

**Publication 26 (1977) — origin of the modern system.** Introduced the three principles later carried forward unchanged in spirit: justification, ALARA ("as low as reasonably achievable," explicitly named here for the first time as a formal principle rather than an informal practice), and dose limitation. Used **dose equivalent H = D·Q·N** (absorbed dose weighted by quality factor Q, a function of LET, and a modifying factor N later dropped) rather than the later radiation-weighting-factor formalism. Recommended a flat **50 mSv/year occupational limit** with no averaging period, and a **5 mSv/year public dose-equivalent limit** for a defined critical group (with an aspirational 0.5 mSv/year population-average target, and an explicit, later-abandoned population genetic dose limit of 5 rem over 30 years). Per-organ risk coefficients were far lower than later editions: total fatal cancer risk ~10⁻²/Sv plus ~4×10⁻³/Sv hereditary risk — roughly half the ~5%/Sv combined figure both ICRP 60 and ICRP 103 later converged on (see below), reflecting the state of A-bomb survivor epidemiology and dosimetry (DS86/DS02 dosimetry revisions) at the time. Publication 26 also introduced the "critical group" concept for public exposure (the most highly exposed subgroup, used as a conservative planning proxy) and a formal limits taxonomy (basic/derived/authorized limits, reference/recording/investigation/intervention levels) that both later editions retain largely unchanged.

**Publication 60 (1990) — the equivalent/effective dose formalism.** Replaced quality factor Q with **radiation weighting factor wR** (photons/electrons wR=1; neutrons a stepped function of energy 5–20, see [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]] for the note on this differing slightly from the 2.5–20 figure elsewhere in this wiki — ICRP 60's function was a step function, later replaced by ICRP 103's continuous function, so figures describing "the ICRP 60 range" and "the ICRP 103 range" are not strictly the same quantity even though both get paraphrased as neutron wR). Introduced **tissue weighting factor wT** (Table 2: gonads 0.20, bone marrow/colon/lung/stomach 0.12 each, bladder/breast/liver/oesophagus 0.05 each, thyroid/skin/bone surface 0.01 each, remainder 0.05) and renamed the doubly-weighted quantity **effective dose** (E = ΣwT·HT), replacing the more cumbersome "effective dose equivalent." Deterministic effects were renamed from "non-stochastic" for clarity. The now-familiar **occupational dose limit of 20 mSv/year averaged over 5 years (max 50 mSv in any single year)** was introduced here, replacing Publication 26's flat 50 mSv/year — derived from an explicit multi-attribute detriment analysis (Table 5: comparing annual doses of 10/20/30/50 mSv against lifetime attributable-death probability, years of life lost, and aggregated detriment) that concluded a lifetime effective dose of ~1 Sv, received moderately uniformly, represented the boundary between "tolerable" and "unacceptable" — this derivation is the direct ancestor of the same limit still in force in Publication 103 today. Also introduced the eye lens (150 mSv/year) and skin (500 mSv/year, averaged over 1 cm²) equivalent-dose limits, and a pregnant-worker abdominal-surface supplementary limit (2 mSv for the remainder of pregnancy after declaration) — all three retained essentially unchanged through Publication 103. Explicitly recommends **no dose limits for medical exposure of patients** (justification and optimization apply instead), a position also retained unchanged since.

**Publication 103 (2007) — the current recommendations.** Explicitly states it does not represent a fundamental policy change from Publication 60 — its own Executive Summary states cancer risk estimates "have not changed appreciably in the past 16 years" and the combined nominal risk coefficient remains ~5%/Sv. What it changes: replaces ICRP 60's stepped neutron wR function with a **continuous function of neutron energy** (Eq. 4.3, peaking near 20 around 1 MeV, ~2.5 at both energy extremes), adds a wR=2 value for **charged pions** (not covered at all in Publication 60), and revises tissue weighting factors to the set already detailed in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]] (breast rising 0.05→0.12, gonads falling 0.20→0.08, reflecting updated cancer/heritable-risk distribution across organs, not a change in total risk). Replaces mathematical/stylized computational phantoms (Fisher-Snyder/ADAM-EVA-type models, already covered generally in [[protocol_ICRU-48-phantoms]]) with **voxel-based reference computational phantoms built from actual tomographic images** (Reference Male and Reference Female, sex-averaged to compute effective dose for a single "Reference Person") — a direct instance of the mathematical-vs-tomographic-model trade-off already discussed in [[protocol_ICRU-48-phantoms]], here resolved in favor of tomographic models for the specific purpose of defining international reference dose-conversion coefficients. Reframes the practices/interventions distinction as three **exposure situations** — planned, emergency, existing — applying justification and optimization universally across all three, with dose limits continuing to apply only to planned exposure. Explicitly retains, unchanged, the effective/equivalent dose limits from Publication 60 (occupational 20 mSv/yr averaged over 5 yr, public 1 mSv/yr, eye 150 mSv/yr, skin/hands/feet 500 mSv/yr).

**What stayed constant across all three editions, and what that implies.** The three core principles (justification, optimization/ALARA, dose limitation), the linear-no-threshold assumption for stochastic effects at low dose, and the basic architecture of occupational/public/medical exposure categories have not changed since 1977 — the Commission's own stated design goal, explicit in the Publication 60 preface, is to "maintain as much stability in the recommendations as is consistent with new information." What has changed across the three editions — the specific weighting-factor values, the phantom-modelling method, the exposure-situation taxonomy, and (most substantially) the occupational dose limit itself (50 mSv/yr flat → 20 mSv/yr averaged over 5 yr) — represents genuine scientific/methodological revision, not policy drift. This distinction is useful when reading older (pre-1990, or 1990s-2000s) radiotherapy/protection literature: a cited "50 mSv/year occupational limit" is not an error, it reflects the Publication 26-era standard in force at the time of writing, superseded but not wrong for its era.
