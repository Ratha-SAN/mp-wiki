## Synthesis

**What this report is.** ICRU Report 50, *Prescribing, Recording, and Reporting Photon Beam Therapy* (1993), is the foundational report defining the volume and dose-reporting vocabulary now standard across external-beam radiotherapy: GTV, CTV, PTV, Treated Volume, Irradiated Volume, Organs at Risk, and the ICRU Reference Point/Dose (MP Library/1993_@medphyslib_ICRU_Committee.pdf). It updates and largely supersedes ICRU Report 29 (1978), and is itself later supplemented by ICRU 62 (1999) — see [[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]] — and extended to IMRT by ICRU 83 — see [[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]].

**The volume hierarchy.** Four nested/related volumes are defined, each serving a distinct purpose:
- **Gross Tumor Volume (GTV)** — the gross palpable, visible, or demonstrable extent of the malignant growth, determined by clinical exam and imaging (CT, MRI, ultrasound, radioisotope methods). No GTV exists if the tumor has already been surgically removed.
- **Clinical Target Volume (CTV)** — a tissue volume containing the GTV and/or subclinical microscopic malignant disease that must be eliminated; a purely anatomic-clinical concept defined without regard to treatment technique. A single case may have multiple CTVs at different presumed disease densities (CTV I, CTV II, ...), e.g. gross tumor vs. regional nodal volumes treated to different doses.
- **Planning Target Volume (PTV)** — a geometric concept: the CTV plus a margin accounting for organ/patient movement and beam/setup inaccuracies, selected so the prescribed dose is actually absorbed in the CTV. Unlike GTV/CTV, the PTV is technique-dependent and tied to beam geometry, not a real anatomic boundary — tissue within it may not exactly match what actually receives the planned dose near its border.
- **Treated Volume** and **Irradiated Volume** — the volume enclosed by an isodose surface chosen by the radiation oncologist as appropriate for the treatment aim (Treated Volume), and the volume receiving a dose considered significant relative to normal-tissue tolerance (Irradiated Volume). Both are reported either as an absolute dose value or relative to the prescribed PTV dose. The report's own worked example (four different techniques irradiating the same PTV) shows Treated:Irradiated volume ratios ranging from 1.0:2.4:4.6 up to 1.0:1.1:11.0 depending on beam arrangement — a large, technique-dependent spread in how much normal tissue is exposed for the same target coverage.
- **Organs at Risk (OARs)** — normal tissues whose radiosensitivity may significantly influence planning or prescribed dose, classed by severity (Class I: fatal/severe morbidity; Class II: moderate/mild morbidity; Class III: mild/transient/reversible).

**Dose reporting: the ICRU Reference Point.** Because a full 3-D dose distribution was often impractical to compute or communicate in 1993, the report's central recommendation is a single representative point — the ICRU Reference Point — selected to be clinically relevant/representative of the PTV dose, easy to define unambiguously, physically accurate, and located away from steep dose gradients. Recommended placement: at the intersection of beam axes if that point lies centrally within the PTV; otherwise on the central beam axis, or (for non-coaxial/non-coplanar/off-center-intersection arrangements) at a specially selected point chosen for clinical relevance. The dose at this point is the **ICRU Reference Dose**, always reported together with the PTV maximum dose (Dmax) and minimum dose (Dmin) — this triad ("the dose variation throughout the PTV") is the minimum reporting requirement. A recommended tolerance for planned dose heterogeneity is +7%/−5% of the prescribed dose.

**Three levels of dose evaluation.** The report explicitly scales its reporting ambition to available planning technology, from Level 1 (reference-point dose plus central-axis variation only — adequate for basic/palliative treatment) through Level 2 (dose computed for one or more planes, from CT/MRI sections) to Level 3 (full 3-D dose-volume computation with dose-volume histograms) — a graduated standard rather than a single fixed bar, which matters when comparing dose reporting from different eras or resource settings.

**Additional dose descriptors.** Beyond Dmax/Dmin/reference dose, the report defines Average Dose (mean over discrete lattice points in a volume), Median Dose, and Modal Dose (most frequent value — flagged by the report itself as often non-unique and therefore not useful for reporting), plus **Hot Spots**: volumes outside the PTV receiving >100% of the specified PTV dose, with a minimum clinically meaningful diameter of 15 mm (smaller for small critical organs like eye, optic nerve, larynx) — the same volume-significance threshold applied to Dmax.

**Complex treatments.** For adjacent PTVs (e.g. breast + regional nodes) each PTV is reported independently at its own ICRU Reference Point. For overlapping/nested PTVs (e.g. a "boost" volume within a larger elective volume — the "shrinking field" technique is treated as a series of nested PTVs), dose distributions are summed and reported per-PTV at Level 2/3, or the reference-point dose alone at Level 1.
