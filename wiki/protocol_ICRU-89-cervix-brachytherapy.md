## Synthesis

**What this report is.** ICRU Report 89, *Prescribing, Recording, and Reporting Brachytherapy for Cancer of the Cervix* (prepared with GEC-ESTRO, published June 2016, content dated 2013), replaces the 2-D radiograph/point-based framework of [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]] with a 3-D, MRI-based, adaptive volumetric framework for cervix brachytherapy (MP Library/2016_@medphyslib_ICRU_Committee.pdf). It is a large, clinically detailed report (13 chapters plus 7 full clinical worked-example cases); this page covers its core prescribing/reporting recommendations (target-volume definitions, dose-volume parameters, radiobiological formalism) rather than its imaging-technique and treatment-planning-algorithm chapters in full.

**Why the 1985 framework needed replacing.** [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]]'s pear-shaped 60 Gy reference volume and point A/B system assumed 2-D orthogonal radiograph planning and a geometrically fixed relationship between applicator and tumor. Modern MRI-based adaptive brachytherapy instead visualizes the actual residual tumor and its response to treatment directly, motivating volumetric (not point-based) target and OAR definitions that can adapt fraction-by-fraction as the tumor regresses.

**Adaptive target-volume framework — the central new concept.** Because cervix tumors typically regress substantially (60–80% volume reduction over 4–5 weeks in one cited series, mean 59 cm³ → 13 cm³) during external-beam therapy before brachytherapy begins, this report introduces a genuinely new volume hierarchy built around that regression: the initial GTV (GTV-Tinit, tumor extent at diagnosis) versus the residual GTV at the time of brachytherapy (GTV-Tres, which can range from near-complete resolution up to 30–90 cm³ of persistent disease depending on response pattern — stable disease, partial remission, or complete remission). From GTV-Tres, an adaptive CTV (CTV-Tadapt) is derived, most often termed the high-risk CTV (CTV-THR) for cervix cancer — defined as the whole cervix plus GTV-Tres plus any residual pathologic (e.g. fibrotic, edematous "gray zone") tissue, representing the volume at highest recurrence risk. An intermediate-risk CTV (CTV-TIR) is the initial GTV-Tinit footprint superimposed on the post-treatment anatomy, with margin around CTV-THR — capturing tissue that bore macroscopic disease at diagnosis but has since responded. A low-risk CTV (CTV-TLR) covers the whole parametria/uterus/upper vagina for potential microscopic spread regardless of response. This GTV-Tinit/GTV-Tres/CTV-THR/CTV-TIR three-tier structure has no counterpart in [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]'s single-CTV external-beam framework — it is specific to brachytherapy's late-course, tumor-response-informed timing.

**No PTV margin in intracavitary brachytherapy.** In sharp contrast to [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]]/[[protocol_ICRU-62-photon-beam-therapy-supplement]]/[[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]]'s external-beam PTV-margin doctrine, this report explicitly recommends against adding a PTV margin around the brachytherapy CTV in routine practice — because the extreme dose gradient around intracavitary sources means a PTV margin would either be dosimetrically meaningless (if the CTV moves within it, dose coverage isn't guaranteed the way it is in a flat external-beam dose region) or would systematically under-report the true CTV dose (since the added margin volume sits at a lower dose than the CTV itself). CTV dose, not PTV dose, is reported as the primary target metric — a genuine, source-documented divergence in planning philosophy from the rest of the ICRU photon-beam series, analogous to how [[protocol_ICRU-91-stereotactic]] similarly departs from the standard IMRT homogeneity convention for a different physical reason.

**Point A redefined, not abandoned.** Rather than dropping the historical Manchester point A (see [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]]), this report gives it a fixed, applicator-based geometric definition — 2 cm cranial to the upper surface of the vaginal applicator part, and 2 cm left/right lateral to the intrauterine tandem — decoupling it from the historical radiographic ambiguity. Point A is retained specifically because its reporting is independent of target-volume contouring, making it a comparable cross-department/cross-study metric even where volumetric planning is unavailable; the report explicitly frames it as a bridge quantity between historical and modern series, not as the primary target-dose metric.

**Dose-volume parameters for target: D98%, D90%, D50%.** For CTV-THR, D98% (near-minimum dose, ≥98% of the volume) is the primary recommended reporting parameter — chosen over D100% because absolute minimum dose is highly sensitive to volume-reconstruction and dose-sampling uncertainty, matching the same D98%-over-Dmin rationale already established in [[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]]. D90% is reported as more robust to random contouring uncertainty but can look artificially favorable if the dose gradient is steep within the volume. D50% (median) is reported for research purposes given its large radiobiological uncertainty at these high near-source doses (discussed below). The same three parameters are recommended for CTV-TIR in clinical trials. For GTV-Tres specifically — the volume immediately adjacent to the applicator, receiving the highest doses of all — D98% is the primary recommended parameter; D90%/D50% are flagged research-only, since EQD2 calculations at these extreme absorbed doses exceed the range in which the underlying LQ model's parameters have been validated (a brachytherapy-specific instance of the general LQ-high-dose caution already flagged in [[concept_fractionation]] and [[protocol_HyTEC-dose-constraints]]).

**OAR reporting: small absolute-volume dose points, not whole-organ DVH alone.** Bladder, rectum, sigmoid, and bowel are reported using D0.1cm³ and D2cm³ — near-maximum dose to small (0.1 cm³ and 2 cm³) high-dose sub-volumes of the organ wall, adjacent to the applicator — rather than a single Dmax point (which the report notes is unreliable given 3-D reconstruction/sampling uncertainty) or whole-organ mean dose alone (which dilutes the clinically relevant near-source hotspot). These small-volume high-dose regions are the direct 3-D volumetric descendant of the point-based bladder/rectal reference points in [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]] — same clinical motivation (bleeding, fistula, ulceration risk near the applicator), now reported as absolute-volume near-maximum dose rather than a single anatomically-defined point. The report cautions that D0.1cm³/D2cm³ from a single fraction cannot be validly compared across schedules with substantially different EBRT:brachytherapy dose ratios without EQD2 correction, since the DVH shape (not just the summary point) changes with that ratio.

**EQD2/EQDX radiobiological formalism for combining EBRT and brachytherapy.** Because brachytherapy fraction doses (often 6–7 Gy/fraction, HDR) are far outside the conventional 2 Gy/fraction range, the report recommends reporting total absorbed dose, dose rate, and fraction/pulse size in Gy with no biological correction as the baseline (Level 1) requirement, with EQD2 conversion (Level 2, using consensus α/β = 10 Gy for tumor/early-responding endpoints and α/β = 3 Gy for late OAR effects — bladder, rectum, sigmoid, small bowel — and recovery half-time T1/2 = 1.5 h for both) recommended wherever available, so that re-derivation is possible if radiobiological parameters are later revised. The report explicitly flags substantial uncertainty in these consensus α/β and T1/2 values (revised analyses suggested T1/2 as short as 0.5 h for late effects and 0.25 h for tumor control, but the report recommends staying with the original, more conservative GEC-ESTRO assumptions pending better clinical validation) — a caution structurally similar to the unresolved LQ-breakdown discrepancy already flagged in [[concept_fractionation]], here specific to brachytherapy recovery kinetics rather than dose-per-fraction threshold. Equivalent Uniform Dose (EUD) is presented for the target (weighting factors ~1.15–1.30 depending on prescription isodose and dose rate) but explicitly not recommended for OAR reporting, given unproven formalism validity for the sharply inhomogeneous OAR dose distributions typical of brachytherapy.

**Level 1/2/3 reporting structure.** Mirrors the graduated approach already used in [[protocol_ICRU-50-photon-beam-therapy]] and [[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]]: Level 1 (minimum) requires the raw dose-delivery pattern (LDR/HDR/PDR-specific: absorbed dose, dose rate, fraction/pulse count and timing) plus total treatment time and EQD2 where available; Level 2 (advanced) adds EQD2 for defined biological endpoints with explicit α/β and T1/2 assumptions; Level 3 (research) adds full DVH parameters and explicit NTCP/TCP modeling.

## Cross-links

Directly supersedes [[protocol_ICRU-38-gynae-intracavitary]] for cervix cancer brachytherapy specifically (2-D point/reference-volume framework → 3-D adaptive volumetric framework); ICRU 38's methodology remains relevant for centers/eras without volumetric imaging, and its bladder/rectal reference-point rationale is the direct conceptual ancestor of this report's D0.1cm³/D2cm³ small-volume OAR parameters. Shares the DVH/near-max/near-min dose-reporting vocabulary of [[protocol_ICRU-83-IMRT]] (D98%/D2%) and [[protocol_ICRU-91-stereotactic]] (absolute small-volume dose points over percentage-volume points, for the same small-volume/steep-gradient reason). The EQD2/α-β/T1/2 formalism connects directly to [[concept_fractionation]]'s BED/NTD2 framework and its unresolved LQ-model-breakdown discrepancy, and to [[technique_re-irradiation]]'s cumulative-dose tracking (this report's EQDX notation for adding doses from different radiation types/schedules is the general form of the EBRT+brachytherapy-specific case). Relevant to [[concept_normal-tissue-response]]'s serial/parallel OAR framework, though this report's small-absolute-volume near-maximum approach is a distinct reporting convention developed specifically for the steep near-source dose gradients of brachytherapy.
