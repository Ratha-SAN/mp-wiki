## Synthesis

**The three-tier IAEA Safety Standards Series structure.** SF-1 *Fundamental Safety Principles* (2006) sits at the top: it states the fundamental safety objective and ten safety principles, forming the basis for everything below. Below it sit **Safety Requirements** (General Safety Requirements Parts 1-7, covering the whole standards programme regardless of facility type, plus Specific Safety Requirements for nuclear power plants/research reactors/fuel cycle facilities/waste disposal/transport) — these are binding-style "shall" statements. Below that sit **Safety Guides**, which provide "should"-level recommendations on how to comply with the Requirements. GSR Part 3 (this page's second source) is General Safety Requirements Part 3 within this structure, sitting alongside GSR Part 1 (Governmental, Legal and Regulatory Framework), Part 2 (Leadership and Management), Part 4 (Safety Assessment), Part 5 (Predisposal Waste Management), Part 6 (Decommissioning), and Part 7 (Emergency Preparedness and Response).

**SF-1 (2006) — the ten fundamental safety principles.** SF-1 consolidated three earlier separate Safety Fundamentals texts (on nuclear installation safety, radioactive waste management safety, and radiation protection/source safety, issued 1993-1995) into one unified set. States the fundamental safety objective — "to protect people and the environment from harmful effects of ionizing radiation" — and ten principles: (1) Responsibility for safety rests with the person/organization responsible for the facility/activity (the "licensee"), non-delegable; (2) Role of government — an effective legal/governmental framework including an independent regulatory body must exist; (3) Leadership and management for safety, including safety culture; (4) Justification — facilities/activities must yield an overall benefit (for medical exposure specifically, justification is considered first for the procedure generically, then patient-by-patient, relying on clinical judgement — directly underpins ICRP's justification principle already documented in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]]); (5) Optimization of protection — highest level of safety reasonably achievable (ALARA, judged against number of people exposed, likelihood, dose magnitude/distribution, and economic/social/environmental factors); (6) Limitation of risks to individuals — dose/risk limits as a backstop, since justification and optimization alone don't guarantee no individual bears unacceptable risk; (7) Protection of present and future generations, including radioactive waste management; (8) Prevention of accidents, primarily via **defence in depth** (multiple independent, redundant levels of protection so no single failure causes harm); (9) Emergency preparedness and response; (10) Protective actions to reduce existing or unregulated radiation risks (radon, orphan sources, legacy contamination) must themselves be justified and optimized, not applied automatically. All ten principles trace directly to the framework already summarized in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]] and elaborated in [[standard_ICRP-recommendations-history]] — SF-1 is the IAEA's own binding-style restatement of the same underlying ICRP-derived philosophy, explicitly built on ICRP recommendations and UNSCEAR findings per its own Background section.

**GSR Part 3 (2014) — the International Basic Safety Standards (BSS).** Supersedes the 1996 "BSS of 1996" (IAEA Safety Series No. 115), which itself descended from IAEA Safety Series No. 9 (1962, revised 1967 and 1982) — a Basic-Safety-Standards lineage stretching back to 1962, paralleling the ICRP lineage documented separately in [[standard_ICRP-recommendations-history]]. The 2014 edition was restructured specifically to align with **ICRP Publication 103's exposure-situation taxonomy** (planned/emergency/existing exposure situations, already documented in [[standard_ICRP-recommendations-history]]'s Publication 103 discussion) — Sections 3, 4, and 5 of GSR Part 3 correspond exactly to these three situations, each with generic requirements plus occupational/public/(for planned situations only) medical-exposure-specific requirements. Within planned exposure situations, GSR Part 3 introduces the **dose constraint** as a distinct tool from the dose limit: a dose constraint is a prospective boundary condition used *during* optimization (set per-source, not itself a compliance threshold — exceeding it triggers follow-up review, not automatic non-compliance), whereas a **reference level** serves the analogous optimization role in emergency and existing exposure situations. Neither dose constraints nor reference levels are dose limits; GSR Part 3's own Requirement 12 (§3.26-3.28) and Schedule III carry the actual numerical dose limits (Schedule III itself, pp.132-370 of the document, was not read in this pass — see gap note below).

**A confirmed dose-limit update: eye lens equivalent dose.** GSR Part 3's development history (§1, Background) documents that in April 2011, after the GSR Part 3 draft was already in late-stage review, ICRP issued a statement on tissue reactions recommending a **new occupational equivalent-dose limit for the lens of the eye: 20 mSv/year, averaged over defined 5-year periods, with no single year exceeding 50 mSv** — replacing the previous 150 mSv/year limit. The IAEA's Commission on Safety Standards formally approved this new limit for GSR Part 3 on 12 July 2011 after Member State consultation. This **confirms and resolves** the "(under revision)" flag already carried on [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]]'s dose-limit table since 2026-08-03 — that page is being updated accordingly (see its History entry for today's date). This is not a contradiction between sources; it is the actual outcome of a revision this wiki had already flagged as pending, now documented from the primary regulatory-standard source.

**What was not read in this pass.** SF-1 was read in full (all ten principles, complete document, 19 pages). For GSR Part 3, only the front matter, Background/Introduction (§1, establishing the exposure-situation taxonomy, dose-constraint/reference-level distinction, and the eye-lens dose-limit history) and the Contents/Requirements list (Requirements 1-52 across Sections 2-5) were read — the actual numbered Requirements' operative text (Sections 2-5 body, pp.18-104) and all four Schedules (Exemption/Clearance, Sealed Source Categories, **Dose Limits for Planned Exposure Situations** [Schedule III, pp.132-370 — the actual numeric dose-limit table], Emergency Preparedness Criteria) were NOT read. This is a substantial gap: GSR Part 3 is the binding regulatory-standard source for dose limits that this wiki has so far only sourced secondhand (via the IAEA radiobiology handbook in [[standard_ICRP-radiation-protection]]) or via the ICRP recommendations themselves (in [[standard_ICRP-recommendations-history]]) — flagged for future ingest rather than treated as complete.
