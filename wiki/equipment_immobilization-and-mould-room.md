## Synthesis

**Scope of this page.** Covers patient immobilization equipment and mould room equipment as specified in WHO/IAEA's *Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment* (2021), Sections 3.8–3.9 (MP Library/Technical specifications of radiotherapy equipment for cancer treatment.pdf). Both are EBRT-specific ancillary equipment categories, closely linked: immobilization equipment holds the patient reproducibly in position, while mould room equipment fabricates the patient-specific beam-shaping devices (blocks, cut-outs) that some LINAC configurations still require.

**Immobilization equipment.** Site-specific by design — the report tabulates distinct equipment for brain (three-point thermoplastic mask with low-density CT-compatible base frame plus high-density head rests), head and neck (five-point thermoplastic mask, similar base frame), breast (angled breast board, up to 25°, with arm/wrist support), thorax (wing board with hand grips), pelvis (vacuum bags, 70×100 cm), and paediatric patients (both three- and five-point thermoplastic masks plus dedicated supine/prone paediatric headrests, and smaller 70×70 cm vacuum bags) — reflecting that a single generic immobilization solution is not adequate across treatment sites. Thermoplastic masks are explicitly single-use consumables requiring ongoing supply, distinct from the reusable base frames/boards. Ancillary items (Table 5, second part): contoured knee/feet supports, a water bath or hot-air oven for heating thermoplastic material before moulding, a compressor for vacuum-bag inflate/deflate cycles, radio-opaque skin markers (for both CT and kV planar imaging visibility), multiple lock bars (the mechanical interface locking immobilization devices to the couch's indexing system), bolus (0.3–2.0 cm thickness range, chosen per photon/electron energy — see [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]] for beam energy context), and lead gonad/eye shields. The report notes prone setups (used for some brain, whole-spine, breast, and pelvis treatments) require their own dedicated immobilization equipment and should only be attempted after consultation with an experienced department, given the added setup-reproducibility and patient-comfort challenge relative to the standard supine position.

**The couch-indexing dependency.** Every immobilization item in this section is explicitly required to be compatible with the department's treatment-couch indexing system via lock bars — the same indexing-compatibility requirement already noted for the CT simulator and conventional simulator in [[equipment_simulators-and-imaging]]. This is a genuine cross-cutting procurement constraint the report flags repeatedly: immobilization equipment, simulator couches, and treatment couches must be indexed to a common system (preferably identical across simulation and treatment rooms) or patient setup reproducibility — the entire purpose of immobilization — is compromised. At least one full base-frame/board set is needed per simulator room and per treatment room, meaning immobilization equipment procurement scales with room count, not just with patient volume.

**Mould room equipment — when it's needed.** Required for cobalt-60 teletherapy practice generally (since cobalt-60 units use shielding blocks rather than an MLC — see [[equipment_LINAC-and-cobalt60-teletherapy]]), for electron beam treatments on any unit (LMPA cut-outs define the electron field at the applicator end), and even for MLC-equipped LINACs in cases still requiring a supplementary customized block. Block/cut-out shape data must transfer from the TPS either electronically (DICOM) or via a printed template at a specified source distance (usually isocentre plane) for manual tracing.

**Mould room equipment — specification.** A fume cupboard (integrated air-cleaning system — reflecting the toxicity of molten low-melting-point alloy, LMPA, fumes) with a melting pot/alloy dispenser (temperature control to 120°C, ≥50 kg alloy capacity) and an aluminium cooling plate for the LMPA (bismuth/lead/tin/cadmium alloy, 70°C melting point). Foam blocks (styrofoam — 2 cm thick for electron cut-outs, 7 cm thick for photon blocks, reflecting the different beam-divergence geometry each requires) are shaped using a hot-wire cutter: a perpendicular-cut cutter for electron cut-outs (handling up to 25×25 cm foam) and a divergent-cut cutter with adjustable arm height/source point for photon beam blocks (handling up to 7×30×30 cm foam) — the divergent cutter must replicate the beam's actual geometric divergence from the source so the resulting block correctly shadows the beam at the treatment distance, not just at the block plane. QA is per-patient: each fabricated block/cut-out shape is compared against a TPS- or simulator-image-generated template before clinical use.
