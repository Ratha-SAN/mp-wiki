## Synthesis

**What this report is.** IAEA Technical Reports Series No. 398 (Rev. 1), *Absorbed Dose Determination in External Beam Radiotherapy: An International Code of Practice for Dosimetry Based on Standards of Absorbed Dose to Water* (2024; first edition 2000), is the current international code of practice for reference dosimetry, replacing [[protocol_TRS-277-absorbed-dose-photon-electron]]'s air-kerma-based formalism with a simpler, more accurate absorbed-dose-to-water-based formalism, and extending scope from photons/electrons to also cover proton and light-ion beams (MP Library/TRS 398 Rev1 2024.pdf). This 2024 revision updates the 2000 first edition to incorporate new ICRU key dosimetry data, modern Monte Carlo-derived correction factors, and small-field/FFF (flattening-filter-free) photon beam guidance, while stating explicitly that it introduces only small differences in determined dose relative to the first edition.

**Why the field moved away from air-kerma-based calibration.** TRS-277's formalism required converting a ⁶⁰Co air-kerma calibration factor NK into an absorbed-dose-to-air chamber factor ND via correction factors km (chamber wall non-air-equivalence) and katt (wall attenuation/scatter) — steps shown to contribute chamber-to-chamber variability not accounted for by generic tabulated values, and to introduce combined standard uncertainties of up to ~4% in some estimates. This report's headline rationale for switching to ND,w-based calibration: (1) **reduced uncertainty** — a primary standard for absorbed dose to water can be established directly in accelerator beams (not just ⁶⁰Co), letting individual-chamber energy dependence be measured experimentally rather than assumed generic, and chamber-to-chamber NDw/NK differences of up to 0.8% (measured by BIPM) are eliminated by calibrating each chamber directly in terms of the quantity actually used clinically; (2) **a more robust system of primary standards** — absorbed-dose-to-water standards (water calorimetry, graphite calorimetry, chemical dosimetry, ionometry) rest on independent physical principles rather than all being ionization-chamber-based like air-kerma standards, so their mutual agreement is more meaningful; (3) **a simpler formalism** — replacing TRS-277's multi-factor km/katt/g/(sw,air) chain with a single beam-quality correction factor kQ,Qo.

**Core formalism.** Absorbed dose to water at the reference depth: Dw,Q = MQ·ND,w,Qo·kQ,Qo, where MQ is the fully corrected chamber reading at the user's beam quality Q, ND,w,Qo is the chamber's absorbed-dose-to-water calibration coefficient at the reference (calibration) quality Qo, and kQ,Qo corrects for the difference in chamber response between the reference quality Qo and the user's actual beam quality Q (kQ,Qo ≡ ND,w,Q/ND,w,Qo by definition). This directly mirrors TRS-277's Bragg-Gray equation Dw(Peff) = M·ND·(sw,air)u·pu, but folds every quality-dependent correction into the single factor kQ,Qo rather than requiring the user to separately look up and multiply stopping-power ratios and perturbation factors.

**The intermediate-quality trick.** A practical data-management problem: if reference chambers are calibrated directly at accelerator beam qualities (rather than only ⁶⁰Co), a full two-dimensional table of kQ,Qo would be needed for every possible (Q, Qo) pair per chamber type. The report solves this by defining an "intermediate quality" Qint (⁶⁰Co for photon beams; R50 = 7.5 g/cm² for electron beams) purely as a bookkeeping device — no actual measurement is made at Qint — so that kQ,Qo = kQ,Qint / kQo,Qint, reducing the data requirement to a single one-dimensional table per chamber type regardless of which quality was actually used for calibration.

**Beam quality specifiers — unchanged in kind from TRS-277, refined in detail.** Photon beams: still TPR20,10, now with an explicitly stated valid range (~0.6 to 0.8, corresponding to beam energies below 25 MV; excludes CyberKnife-type and MR-linac beams). Electron beams: still R50 (half-value depth), now over an extended range (4–25 MeV, R50 = 1.4–10 g/cm²). New in this revision: proton beams (50–250 MeV, practical range Rp = 0.25–37 g/cm²) and light ions up to neon (practical range 2–30 g/cm², corresponding to 85–430 MeV/u for carbon ions) — an extension of scope with no TRS-277 counterpart, since TRS-277 predates clinical proton/ion therapy's current scale.

**Updated key physical constants.** Following the ICRU's 2016 key-data report, this revision uses mean excitation energies I = 85.7 eV (air), 81 eV (graphite), 78 eV (water), and confirms **Wair = 33.97 eV for electrons** — unchanged from the value already adopted in [[protocol_TRS-277-absorbed-dose-photon-electron]] (constant above ~10 keV) — while introducing new values for protons (34.44 eV) and carbon ions (34.71 eV), extending the W-value framework of [[protocol_ICRU-31-W-value]] into particle types that report did not cover. Updated photon cross-sections (from the PENELOPE Monte Carlo system's 2014 renormalized photoelectric-effect database) changed air-kerma standards for kilovoltage X-ray and ⁶⁰Co beams by up to ~1%, but left water-calorimetry-based absorbed-dose-to-water standards essentially unchanged — illustrating concretely why an absorbed-dose-to-water primary standard is more robust to this kind of underlying-physics revision than an air-kerma standard built on the same cross-section data.

**Reference-class ionization chambers.** The report formalizes a "reference class chamber" specification (cavity volume 0.1–1 cm³, internal diameter ≤~7 mm, internal length ≤~25 mm for cylindrical chambers) — a tightened, explicitly-named performance tier beyond TRS-277's general chamber-construction guidance, reflecting a decade-plus of accumulated evidence about which chamber designs give reliably low chamber-to-chamber variability. Plastic (A-150, nylon) walled chambers are now explicitly discouraged as reference instruments due to hygroscopic response variability; graphite-walled chambers are preferred for long-term stability.

**Contrast with AAPM TG-51 — same purpose, parallel but distinct lineage.** AAPM's TG-51 protocol (Almond et al., *Medical Physics* 26:1847, 1999) is the equivalent North American reference-dosimetry protocol, published the year before TRS-398's first edition, and independently arrived at essentially the same conceptual move — an absorbed-dose-to-water calibration factor traceable to national primary standards (NIST in the US, NRCC in Canada), with a single beam-quality correction factor kQ = ND,w^Q/ND,w^60Co. The core formalism, Dw^Q = M·kQ·ND,w^60Co, is structurally identical to TRS-398's Dw,Q = MQ·ND,w,Qo·kQ,Qo. Differences are mainly in beam-quality specifiers and practical convention rather than underlying physics: TG-51 specifies photon beam quality via %dd(10)x (the photon component of percentage depth-dose at 10 cm, correcting for electron contamination using a lead-foil measurement above 10 MV) rather than TRS-398's TPR20,10 — the two indices are correlated but not identical, and TG-51's %dd(10)x requires a distinct experimental protocol (lead-foil placement, and empirical correction formulas Eq. (13)/(14) in the TG-51 paper) with no TRS-398 counterpart. For electron beams, TG-51 uses the same R50 quality specifier as TRS-398, but factors kQ = Pgr^Q·kR50 into a gradient-correction component (Pgr^Q, chamber-specific, measured by the user) and a beam-quality component (kR50), and additionally introduces kecal, a fixed "photon-electron conversion factor" per chamber model that lets a single ⁶⁰Co calibration be converted directly into an electron-beam calibration factor without needing electron-beam calibration data at all — a data-reduction trick with a similar goal to, but a different mechanism from, TRS-398's intermediate-quality Qint approach. TG-51 also mandates water-only reference dosimetry (explicitly disallowing plastic phantoms for absolute calibration, only for more frequent QA checks against an annually-verified water-based transfer factor) — a stricter, simpler rule than TRS-398's more permissive plastic-phantom scaling framework (see [[protocol_TRS-277-absorbed-dose-photon-electron]]'s Table III-style plastic/water range-scaling, which TRS-398 retains and extends). Both protocols are actively used today — TG-51 predominates in North America, TRS-398 internationally — and a physicist moving between the two literatures needs to track which beam-quality index (TPR20,10 vs. %dd(10)x) and which kQ dataset a given published value refers to, since the two are not numerically interchangeable without the appropriate correlation curve.
