## Synthesis

**Overview.** Biological dosimetry estimates absorbed radiation dose from biomarkers in peripheral blood, used alongside or instead of physical dosimetry after suspected/known exposure (raw/Radiobiology Textbook.pdf, Ch.8 §8.6.1). This page gives the fuller methods survey behind the brief dicentric-assay summary already in [[concept_dna-damage-and-repair]].

**Dicentric chromosome assay (DCA) — the gold standard.** Stimulated peripheral-blood lymphocytes are cultured to metaphase and scored for dicentric chromosomes, which are almost exclusively radiation-induced (background ~0.5–1 per 1000 cells) (Ch.8 §8.6.2). Detection limit ~100 mGy (acute, low-LET, 500–1000 cells scored); dose-response is linear-quadratic for low-LET radiation up to ~5 Gy, linear for high-LET. Dicentric-bearing lymphocytes have a mean lifetime of 0.6–3 years, so old or protracted exposures require correction to avoid dose underestimation. Labor-intensive and requires trained staff; triage scoring (30 dicentrics or 50 cells) and automated/high-throughput platforms (RABiT — Rapid Automated Biodosimetry Tool, imaging flow cytometry) have been developed for mass-casualty scenarios (Ch.8 §8.6.2.1).

**Cytokinesis-block micronucleus (CBMN) assay.** Scores micronuclei (excluded chromosome fragments or whole chromosomes) in binucleated cells after cytochalasin-B-blocked cytokinesis (Fenech & Morley, 1985). Higher background rate than dicentrics (0–40 MN/1000 binucleated cells) so somewhat less sensitive (~200–300 mGy detection limit); FISH pancentromeric probing distinguishes whole-chromosome (centromere-positive) from fragment (centromere-negative) micronuclei, improving low-dose sensitivity, since most radiation-induced MN are centromere-negative. Less radiation-specific than dicentrics (influenced by age, sex, lifestyle, other mutagens) but simpler, faster, and more amenable to automation/training of inexperienced staff (Ch.8 §8.6.2.2).

**FISH translocation analysis.** Chromosome-painting probes quantify stable translocations, useful because (unlike dicentrics) translocations persist for years, making this the method of choice for retrospective/remote-past exposure assessment. M-FISH (genome-wide, multiple fluorophores) and mBAND (chromosome-specific banding) extend detection to complex/intra-chromosomal rearrangements. Detection limit ~250 mGy for uniform whole-body low-LET exposure; does not work well for partial-body exposure; background translocation rate must be subtracted and is itself influenced by age/smoking status (Ch.8 §8.6.2.3).

**Premature chromosome condensation (PCC) assay.** Fuses G0 lymphocytes to mitotic CHO cells (via PEG) or chemically induces condensation in G2 cells (okadaic acid/calyculin A) to visualize chromosome damage directly in interphase cells, without requiring mitotic stimulation — useful specifically for high-dose accident scenarios (>4 Gy) where DCA/CBMN become problematic, and can distinguish whole- vs. partial-body exposure within ~3 hours post-irradiation. Requires availability of fresh/frozen mitotic cells and cell-fusion expertise, limiting widespread use (Ch.8 §8.6.2.4).

**Molecular methods.** γH2AX foci assay: indirectly quantifies DSBs via phospho-specific antibody staining of the H2AX histone variant (see [[concept_dna-damage-and-repair]] for γH2AX as a DSB marker); dose estimate achievable within ~5 hours of blood draw, background ~0.3 foci/cell, linear dose-response — but foci decay follows DSB repair kinetics, so results are highly time-since-exposure dependent (minimum detectable dose rises from ~1 mGy at 1 hour post-exposure to ~0.5 Gy at 2 days), making this better suited as a qualitative triage indicator than a precise dosimeter; dual-staining with 53BP1 improves low-dose sensitivity (Ch.8 §8.6.3.1). Gene-expression biodosimetry: quantifies radiation-responsive transcriptional changes (qRT-PCR or microarray) in peripheral lymphocytes, sensitive from ~100 mGy to 5 Gy in ex vivo calibration, with ferredoxin reductase (FDRX) as the most promising single marker identified so far — but single-gene estimation is unreliable given the complexity of the radiation response, so multi-gene panel signatures are the active research direction; still considered experimental, with health status/age/sex confounding results (Ch.8 §8.6.3.2).
