# Fractionation

Splitting a course of treatment exploits differences in repair capacity between
tumour and late-responding normal tissue — the four Rs: repair, reassortment,
repopulation, reoxygenation.

## Equivalent dose

$$
\mathrm{EQD}_2 = D \cdot \frac{d + \alpha/\beta}{2 + \alpha/\beta}
$$

derived directly from the [[Linear Quadratic Model#Biologically effective dose|BED]]
expression.

## Schedules

| Schedule | Fraction size | Typical use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conventional | 1.8–2 Gy | Most curative courses |
| Hypofractionated | 2.5–3 Gy | Breast, prostate |
| SBRT | 6–20 Gy | Small targets, steep gradients |

Steep gradients depend on delivery accuracy — see [[IMRT]] and
[[Treatment Planning/Monte Carlo Dose Calculation|Monte Carlo]].

Repopulation kinetics: [[Tumour Doubling Time]].
